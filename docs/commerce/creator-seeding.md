# Creator Seeding — Cold-Start Proof Engine

Written 2026-08-14. Companion to `positioning.md` (voice + claims) and
`instagram-launch.md` (the account itself).

## Why this exists

The warm plan — five friends, five honest reactions, photos of real kids reading —
was never a revenue plan. It was a **proof-manufacturing** plan. With no warm network,
the substitute is strangers who have audiences: gift a free custom book to a
micro-creator, no obligation to post, and some fraction post anyway.

This works better here than seeding usually does, because the free thing is a book
about *their own child*. Most seeding fails because the creator is indifferent to the
item. Nobody is indifferent to their kid as the main character.

**Cost:** ~1 attended hour per book (see the corrected time model). At 15–20 books
that's 15–20 hours over a month, and it doubles as the pipeline reps the shop needs
anyway.

**Expected yield:** roughly 35% accept, and about half of those post. Twenty messages
≈ 4–5 pieces of public proof. Budget for ghosting.

---

## The decision that comes before the first DM

**Settle your AI-illustration disclosure sentence, in writing, and be willing to say
it in a public comment thread.**

Through 2025–26 the kidlit world hardened on this: Penguin Random House and Macmillan
banned AI-generated submissions, Amazon KDP requires AI disclosure, and New Zealand's
top book award disqualified two authors over AI cover imagery. The standing objection
— models trained on living illustrators' work without consent — is well understood
inside the niche.

Consequences, in order:

1. **Bookstagram is the highest-risk audience**, because it overlaps heavily with
   working authors and illustrators. A creator who posts your book and gets accused
   of shilling AI slop deletes the post and remembers.
2. **Volunteering beats concealing.** *"The words are written by a human; the
   illustrations are generated and then reviewed page by page"* is defensible. Silence
   followed by a comment-section discovery is not.
3. **Never seed a working picture-book illustrator.** Unforced error.
4. Homeschool and gentle-parenting audiences are materially less reactive than
   bookstagram. **So seed homeschool first** — see the sequence below.

This is also why `positioning.md` says "curated, not AI slop" rather than "no AI."
The claim is human craft and judgment, not the absence of tools. Say that.

| # | Move | Why in this order |

"mom life" lifestyle (audience follows the person, not recommendations; already
monetised; nobody in-band anyway) · anyone whose kids are 0–3 or 9+.

## Finding more

Published lists rank by influence, so their tops are 100K–600K accounts. **Your band
lives in the tail** — Feedspot's "Show 49 to 1053" expander is the richest free vein.

**The highest-signal move: mine recurring-series hashtags, not topic hashtags.**
#bookstagram has 50M+ posts and is useless. Micro accounts invent and repeat their own
weekly-series tags, and those tags are almost purely small accounts. Real examples
from the bios above:

`#SundayBedtimeStory` · `#BooksWithBreakfast` · `#FridayLibraryFind` ·
`#bookmatchmonday` · `#creatingreadingclassrooms`

Search those, read the participating accounts' bios for *their* series tags, repeat.
This compounds.

**Topical tags** — build 15–20 and rotate weekly, always the **Recent** tab, never Top:
#kidsbookstagram #picturebooksofinstagram #readaloud #bedtimestories #raisingreaders
#homeschoolmom #charlottemasonhomeschool #gentleparenting #montessoriathome
#screenfreekids #livingbooks #homeschoolpreschool

**Lateral discovery, cheapest scaling mechanism:**

- **"Suggested for you"** on any profile returns accounts of comparable size in the
  same niche. Chain it.
- **Read their following list.** A 12K bookstagrammer's following list is a curated
  directory of other 5–30K bookstagrammers.
- **Comment sections of the mega-accounts** — the substantive commenters are the micro
  accounts you want.
- Follow 20–30 targets and engage for a week; Explore starts serving the same tier.

**Age filter, before you spend a book:**

1. **Bio first.** Roughly a third state ages outright — "PreK," "kindergarten," "1st
   and 3rd," "littles," "tinies." Reject "0–3" and "tweens/teens."
2. **Grid check.** Does a child of the right age actually appear, and is the child's
   name used publicly? If they never show or name the kid, the payoff — seeing their
   child in the story — is one they can't post about.

## The messages

Voice per `positioning.md`: warm, second person, no exclamation marks, no emoji, no
agency-speak. This niche punishes templates.

### Opening DM

Reference a specific recent post. In a niche this small that single line outperforms
any pitch — it proves you looked.

```
Hi [name] — I saw your [specific post, by name] last week.

I make custom children's storybooks. Not a name swap: the kid becomes the actual
main character, written about who they are, illustrated and narrated.

I'd like to make one for [child's name] for free. No obligation to post anything —
I'd rather you see it and decide.

If you're up for it, all I need is their age and two or three things they're into
right now. Nothing else, and I don't keep it after the book is delivered.
```

**Do not ask for a photo of the child in the first message.** Even though the pipeline
can use one, asking a stranger for a photograph of their kid is the fastest way to get
blocked and screenshotted. Offer photo-based likeness only if *they* raise it, later,
with the retention policy in the same breath.

### If they say yes

```
Brilliant. Three questions and then I'll get out of your way.

1. What's [child]'s name, and how old are they?
2. Two or three things they're deep in right now.
3. One thing that's been sticky for them lately — a transition, a big feeling,
   something they're working through. That's what the story ends up being about.

Picture books you love the look of are useful too, if any come to mind. That's what
the art style gets built from.
```

### On delivery — the disclosure line

Include this every time. It's your legal obligation to explain, and it reads as
professional rather than amateur:

```
Here it is. Two things, no pressure on either.

If you do post it, please mark it as gifted — #gifted or the Paid Partnership tag
is fine. And I won't repost [child]'s pages anywhere without asking you first.
```

### If they ask for a rate

At 15–25K a meaningful minority have a rate card. This answer costs nothing and keeps
the relationship:

```
I can't pay yet — it's just me and this is early. The book's yours regardless.
Post it only if you love it.
```

### If they ask about AI

Answer in one plain sentence, then move on. Don't get defensive and don't over-explain.
Have this settled before you send a single Tier A message.

## Legal and consent

**FTC.** A gifted product creates a "material connection" even with no agreement to
post. If they post, they must disclose — $5 sample and $5,000 deal carry the identical
obligation. Disclosure must be above the "more" fold in captions, not buried in
hashtags, and on-screen throughout in video. Instagram's Paid Partnership label and
TikTok's Branded Content toggle both satisfy it. **The FTC pursues brands for their
creators' non-disclosure**, which is why the line above is non-negotiable.

**Children's likeness.** State laws on monetised child content (IL, CA, MN, UT), a
cultural turn against sharenting, and — most relevant — AI tools that can now do far
more with one uploaded photo than they could two years ago. So:

- No photo request in a cold message.
- State the data policy unprompted. It's a differentiator in this niche, not
  boilerplate.
- **Permission to receive a gift is not permission to market their child.** Never
  repost a creator's child's pages without separate explicit permission.
