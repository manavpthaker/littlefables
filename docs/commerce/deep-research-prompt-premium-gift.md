# Deep Research Prompt — Premium Personalized Book Gift Market

Paste everything below the line into a deep research tool.

---

**Role:** You are a skeptical market analyst. Your job is to try to kill this thesis, not confirm it. Every number needs a named source and year. No top-down "X% of a $YB market" sizing. US market.

**Working hypothesis — treat as revisable, not fixed:** Adults over 50 with disposable income (grandparents, aunts/uncles, godparents) will pay **~$189** for a fully custom personalized children's book — child's likeness, story written for that specific child, premium hardcover — as a milestone gift (first birthday, new sibling, starting kindergarten, adoption day, Christmas) for a child aged 3–9. The motive is proof they paid attention to who the child is, not the book itself. Price is not the objection; being one of six identical gifts is. Secondary: parents in situations where generic books fail (adoption, foster, neurodivergence, hard transitions) at **~$89 digital**. I may revise the offering entirely — start from buyer discovery, and if the evidence says a different offer shape or price wins, say so.

**Already known — do not repeat:** The $25–$47 template tier (Wonderbly, Hooray Heroes, I See Me: name + preset avatar dropped into a fixed story) is mapped. Their dominant Trustpilot complaint is delivery delay around gift dates. Etsy digital personalized books quote 2–7 days with a preview-approval flow as standard.

**Answer these eight questions, in this order:**

1. **Primary persona, from scratch.** Who actually buys premium (>$100) personalized keepsakes for children 3–9? Build 2–3 evidence-based buyer personas, each with: demographics and income, relationship to the child, occasion triggers, where they discover gift ideas (Pinterest, Google, Etsy, Facebook, gift guides, word of mouth), device and tech comfort, decision process (do they consult the parent first? lead time before the occasion?), the emotional job ("no one else could give this"), objections (child-photo privacy, AI-generated art, ordering friction), and digital-vs-physical attitudes. Ground each persona in verbatim buyer language — pull quotes from reviews of premium keepsake sellers, Reddit, Facebook grandparent groups, gift-guide comments. Flag where evidence is thin rather than inventing detail.

2. **Offer discovery — do not assume the current product is right.** Given those personas, what offer shape wins? Test the attributes independently: hardcover vs digital vs both; photo-real likeness vs stylized illustration; fully custom story vs template-plus; human vs AI illustration and disclosure; preview-and-approve vs surprise; gift certificate / wrap / handoff mechanics; turnaround tolerance per occasion. Which attributes do buyers demonstrably pay premiums for in adjacent categories, and which are indifferent? Output: a ranked attribute list with evidence, then 2–3 candidate offer shapes with defensible price bands — even if none of them is the $189 hardcover.

3. **Demand proof at the price.** Who currently sells fully custom (not template) personalized children's books at $100–$400? Find 5–10 real sellers — Etsy bespoke illustrators, indie studios, heirloom-book makers. For each: price, offer shape, review count, estimated monthly sales, turnaround, what reviews praise and complain about. If nobody sustains repeat sales above $150, say so plainly — that is the kill signal.

4. **The gift wallet.** Verified per-occasion gift spend by grandparents and non-parent gift-givers (not lifetime/aggregate headline stats). What do they spend on milestone gifts specifically? Which premium keepsake categories already clear $150–$300 (custom portraits from photos, heirloom jewelry, keepsake boxes) — evidence the price point works in adjacent categories with the same buyer?

5. **TAM / SAM / SOM — bottom-up only.** TAM: US children aged 3–9 × milestone occasions per child per year × share receiving a premium (>$100) gift from a non-parent. SAM: the slice reachable through Etsy, Pinterest, and direct search for personalized keepsakes. SOM: realistic year-1 revenue for a new shop with under 50 reviews, benchmarked against actual trajectories of new premium Etsy sellers. Show all arithmetic and every assumption. Give low / base / high.

6. **Comp set at this tier, not the $30 tier.** Map the bespoke competitors on: price, likeness fidelity (photo-real vs stylized), human vs AI illustration and whether they disclose it, turnaround, revision policy, and how they handle the child's photo after delivery. Identify where "human-reviewed, buyer approves a preview, photos deleted by default" is a real differentiator vs table stakes.

7. **Occasion economics.** Rank first birthday, new sibling, kindergarten, adoption day, and Christmas by search volume, seasonality, and typical gift spend. Which single occasion should a new shop own first, and why?

8. **Secondary persona and the ~$89 digital tier.** Build the persona for parents in adoption / foster / neurodivergence / hard-transition situations the same way as Q1: triggers, discovery channels, language, objections. Is there evidenced demand for situation-specific personalized books? Who serves it now, at what price, and do their reviews actually contain "generic books didn't work for us" language?

**Output format:** Per question: a table of numbers with sources, then max 3 takeaway bullets phrased as "so what / what I'd do." Then close with: (a) the single strongest persona-and-offer combination the evidence supports, and whether it matches the working hypothesis or replaces it, (b) a go/no-go read on $189 hardcover specifically, (c) the 3 riskiest assumptions and the cheapest 2-week test for each, (d) anything found that contradicts the thesis. Under 3,000 words total. Tag every key number: verified / directional / weak.

**Do not cite — previously refuted in my own verification passes:** "$238B/yr grandparent spend," "$3,917/yr per grandchild," "homeschooling grew 40% 2018–2022," "5.2% of K-12 home instruction," "78% of Pinterest users use it for holiday inspiration."
