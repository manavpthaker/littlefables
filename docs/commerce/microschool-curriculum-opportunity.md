# Microschool Curriculum Opportunity

## Working thesis

The stronger opportunity is not a catalog of generic personalized books. It is a small, adult-approved learning artifact made from a group’s real work: a season outdoors, a garden harvest, a service project, a local place, or a shared question. The children supply the observations and language; Little Fables supplies story structure, illustration, read-aloud audio, and a take-home format.

This is a hypothesis to test, not a settled product direction. The first research question is whether leaders already feel a recurring need to preserve or share group learning after a project ends.

## What to test with pilots

1. **Artifact need:** What do families receive after a project now—photos, worksheets, a slideshow, a craft, nothing?
2. **Use case:** Is the story read aloud in the group, taken home, used as a review, or given at year-end?
3. **Input burden:** Can a leader provide a short brief, vocabulary, and a few approved observations without creating homework?
4. **Privacy boundary:** Are first names, fictionalized composites, or no child identifiers acceptable? Treat faces and likenesses as opt-in, never assumed.
5. **Repeatability:** Would the same group commission one artifact per term or only one keepsake?
6. **Budget owner:** Is payment made by the program, participating families, a donor, or a gift sponsor?

## Product ladder to validate

- **Pilot story:** 12–20 illustrated pages, narrated audio, and a printable PDF for one real project.
- **Term set:** Three stories across a season or semester, each tied to a project and vocabulary list.
- **Class anthology:** A group-owned digital book with a short page for each child only when consent and editorial value are clear.
- **Curriculum companion:** Story, discussion prompts, observation scavenger hunt, vocabulary, and a simple reflection page. This is a supplement, not a claimed accredited curriculum.

Do not build the term set or anthology before one pilot shows repeat use. Compare a story-only control against the companion bundle to learn whether the educational scaffolding earns its extra production cost.

## Discovery success criteria

Advance a prospect when a leader identifies a recent shared project, names a specific audience/use, agrees to an adult approval path, and can describe who would fund a repeat. A compliment, a social follow, or willingness to accept a free book is not evidence of demand.

## Research guardrails

Use public program descriptions only to form a fit hypothesis. Verify the current decision-maker, schedule, enrollment, and contact route before outreach. Keep research-only directories separate from pilot candidates, and do not solicit groups that explicitly prohibit business outreach.
