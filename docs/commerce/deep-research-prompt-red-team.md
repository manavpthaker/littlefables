# Deep Research Prompt — Red Team the Whole Business

Paste everything below the line into a deep research tool. Run this after (or alongside) the market prompt and the fulfillment prompt; this one attacks the conclusions.

---

**Role:** You are a red team. Your job is to kill this business — attack every assumption, then rebuild from the bones whatever survives. No cheerleading. Every number needs a named source and year, or must be tagged as an estimate with its arithmetic shown. US market.

**Context:** Solo operator, part-time, selling fully custom personalized children's books. The digital pipeline (story, illustration, buyer preview approval, digital delivery) is built and working — marginal software cost per order is near zero, but human hours per order are not. The goal is **supplemental part-time income**, not venture scale. Assume ~10 hours/week available; show sensitivity at 5 and 15.

**Three models to stress-test against each other:**

- **Model A — original:** $69 digital-only custom book.
- **Model B — current hypothesis:** $189 premium physical hardcover (custom story + likeness, printed qty 1, shipped) with an $89 digital secondary tier for adoption / foster / neurodivergence / hard-transition situations.
- **Model C — challenger:** whatever alternative the evidence actually supports (different price, different mix, different product shape). Construct it; don't force it to resemble A or B.

**Answer these seven questions:**

1. **Bones: unit economics per model.** For each model, build the true per-order picture: human hours (story, art generation and curation, QA, buyer communication, revision rounds — use what custom-portrait and custom-book sellers report, not optimistic guesses), out-of-pocket costs (print, shipping, platform fees, payment fees, ad spend per order), and the resulting **effective hourly rate**. Kill signal: state plainly which models pay below a reasonable freelance floor (~$30/hr) and are therefore a job, not income.

2. **Demand reality at each price.** For a shop with under 50 reviews: realistic monthly order volume at $69, $89, and $189, benchmarked against observable new-seller trajectories in custom books and custom portraits. Attack the $69 point specifically: does it sit in a dead zone — too expensive vs $30 template incumbents, too cheap to signal bespoke? Is there price-elasticity evidence in personalized gifts either way?

3. **The strongest case against each model.** Steelman the attacks: (a) AI commoditization — KidTeller-style apps generate personalized illustrated books for under $1; how fast is "custom book" being commoditized and does "human-crafted" hold a premium? (b) the "proof of attention" motive can be satisfied cheaper; (c) grandparent friction with digital delivery kills A and the $89 tier; (d) revision expectations and refund exposure scale with price and eat Model B's margin in labor; (e) Etsy platform risk — fee changes, search burial, account suspension. Rate each attack: fatal / manageable / weak, with evidence.

4. **Capacity and seasonality math.** Part-time hours cap → max orders/month per model → hard revenue ceiling. Then apply the gift calendar: if 40–60% of annual orders land in the 6 weeks before Christmas (verify the actual concentration for personalized gifts), what does peak-week workload look like, where does turnaround break, and what does a blown Christmas delivery do to a sub-50-review shop?

5. **What solo operators actually earn.** Find real reported outcomes — Etsy seller forums, Reddit (r/EtsySellers and similar), indie-maker writeups — from solo sellers of custom children's books, custom portraits, and comparable made-to-order gifts: revenue, hours, how long they lasted, why the quitters quit. This is the base rate; state it plainly even if it's ugly.

6. **Rebuild from the bones.** Given everything above, construct the most feasible configuration(s) — price, product mix, order volume, hours — to hit supplemental income bands of **$500 / $1,000 / $2,000 per month net of costs**. For each band: which model gets there, what has to be true, and the biggest single point of failure. If no configuration hits $1,000/mo inside 10 hrs/week, say so.

7. **Pre-mortem and kill criteria.** The five most likely ways this fails within 12 months, ranked. For each: the cheapest, fastest falsification test. Then explicit walk-away conditions — "do not proceed / stop doing this if X" — measurable, not vibes.

**Output format:** Per question: a table of numbers with sources, then max 3 takeaway bullets phrased as "so what / what I'd do." Then close with: (a) a verdict table — Models A, B, C on effective hourly rate, realistic monthly net at 10 hrs/week, revenue ceiling, top risk, survivability; (b) the single recommended configuration and why it beat the others; (c) a plain-language go/no-go on the business as a part-time income vehicle at all; (d) the 3 assumptions most likely to be wrong in my own framing of this prompt. Under 3,000 words. Tag every key number: verified / directional / weak / estimate.

**Do not cite — previously refuted in my own verification passes:** "$238B/yr grandparent spend," "$3,917/yr per grandchild," "homeschooling grew 40% 2018–2022," "5.2% of K-12 home instruction," "78% of Pinterest users use it for holiday inspiration."
