# Deep Research Prompt — Final Model Selection & Build-Out

Fourth and final pass. Synthesizes the three August 2026 reports in `docs/commerce/research/`
(market-persona, red-team, fulfillment) and was adversarially audited against them before
finalizing. **Attach or paste all three reports when running this prompt** — they are the
evidence base it adjudicates.

Where the reports agree, this prompt locks the finding. Where they conflict — price, digital's
role, templatization depth, holiday cutoffs, even the hourly floor itself — the researcher must
settle it with fresh evidence, then build the winning model end to end.

---

**Role:** You are a business strategist making a final call, with a skeptic's discipline: named source and year for every number, tag each key figure verified / directional / weak / estimate, show all arithmetic. Three research passes are attached. Your job is NOT to redo them. It is to (1) adjudicate the contradictions between them with fresh primary evidence, (2) pick ONE business model, and (3) build it out completely. Where fresh evidence conflicts with either report, fresh evidence wins. Where my framing below conflicts with the reports, flag it — some derivations are mine, and they are marked. Do not presuppose a winner, including the configurations I name — if the evidence supports a shape none of us proposed, construct it. US market.

**Operator context:** Solo, ~10 hrs/week, supplemental-income goal. Design target **$500/mo net**; $1,000/mo is a Q4 spike, not run-rate; $2,000/mo is ruled out. Buyer lead times: grandparents order 3–4 weeks ahead, godparents 1–2; observed bespoke turnaround runs 20–25 days against the proposed 2-week promise. Surviving ops rules: 2-revision cap, written proof approval before print, email capture on every order.

**Contradictions to adjudicate — this is the core job:**

- **A0. The hourly floor itself.** The red team demands a ~$30/hr freelance floor and a "<$25/hr by order #5 → stop" rule — yet its own recommended model clears only ~$23/hr. Under a strict $30 floor, every named configuration fails. Decide the real floor first (true opportunity cost of these specific hours for this operator), state it, and run the tournament against it.
- **A. Price: $89 vs $165–189.** Market report's case: the $150–400 fully-custom band shows no survivors with provable repeat sales; grandparents' *birthday-gift* range is $50–100 (directional); the wallet clears $150–300 only for heirloom-framed categories (portraits, jewelry) — so $150+ requires borrowing the heirloom-portrait frame. Red team's case: RNBishere is an existence proof ($149 sale / $299 list, 595 sales in ~1 year, solo); Liu et al. 2022 — givers rate higher-priced keepsake gifts as better; raise price, not volume. **My derivation, not the red team's — check it:** at $89 on Etsy with $22–30 print, net before labor ≈ $32–45/order, so a $30/hr floor affords only ~1–1.5 hrs/order. Settle it: forensic teardown of RNBishere (stylized or photo-real, templatization depth, anchoring structure, monthly review velocity, turnaround, inferable hours/order); find 5–10 more sellers with provable velocity across $80–250 and draw the demand curve; resolve the print-cost conflict; then state the max hours/order each price affords at the chosen floor and whether that budget is achievable at credible quality.
- **B. Digital's role — a three-way fight, not two.** Market report: launch a $49 digital-first SKU to build reviews fast. Red team: physical-only base, digital only as a +$29 add-on; digital-first is fatal for gifting. Fulfillment report: the inverse — sell *physical as a $189 add-on on a digital base* to de-risk fulfillment while keeping digital cash flow. Also unresolved: the special-situations product is priced $49 (market), $109 (red team), $89 (original, unproven) across the documents. Settle both: which product is the base, and what the situations tier costs — with comps, not preference.
- **C. Templatization depth.** The market report's winner is semi-custom (a situation-story frame + the child's details); the red team's positioning line is "made from scratch, no templates." These conflict — and templatization is the only labor-compression lever. How templatized can the product be before buyers read it as Wonderbly-at-3x and the anti-duplicate claim collapses? Find review language in semi-custom sellers ("felt generic," "same as her cousin's") vs fully bespoke.
- **D. The narrated app edition.** First **verify** no competitor bundles a printed book with a narrated app edition — do not assume it. If unique: does it raise willingness-to-pay or conversion (any evidence from print+digital-companion bundles in kids' books or adjacent keepsakes)? Test the framing tension: ~60% of parents report screen-time guilt (Lurie Children's 2025, previously verified), so evaluate whether "replaces lower-value screen time" framing lands with a 50+ buyer or whether the app repels them — as a question, not a foregone conclusion. And cost it honestly: per-order narration labor (two voice modes), plus the open-ended hosting/support promise a "living edition" implies from a 10-hr/week operator — including what happens to buyers' editions if the shop closes.
- **E. Holiday cutoff.** Red team: hard Dec 1 cutoff (bespoke labor queue; veterans close customs early). Fulfillment: Dec 10–12 standard / Dec 18 express (print + transit only). The true cutoff = labor queue + production + transit at peak load; derive it rather than adopting either.

**Build the winner out — four workstreams:**

1. **Market position.** Placement against the three competitor classes (template incumbents $30–60, AI apps <$10–40, bespoke commissions $1k+); the one-line positioning claim; launch occasion and expansion sequence (resolving the new-sibling spend gap); expected competitive response within 12 months (an AI app adding "human-edited," Wonderbly adding a premium line) and the counter.
2. **Customer discovery evidence.** Which claims demonstrably move this buyer ("human-written" vs "no AI" vs "photos deleted" vs "one of a kind" vs "arrives on time"); how the listing handles the giver-consults-parent veto; verbatim objection language at the chosen price; discovery channels ranked by persona with realistic CAC ranges.
3. **SWOT and moat clock.** Full SWOT for the winner; one line each on why the losers lost; the moat-erosion clock — what must be built (brand, reviews, email list, occasion ownership) before the human-made premium narrows, with dates.
4. **Business model build-out.** Offer ladder with prices and anchoring; per-order unit economics on the resolved cost base (Etsy stack + Offsite Ads scenario + resolved print cost + narration labor if C4) with an explicit labor budget and what is templatized vs bespoke per order; channel plan and review flywheel to 50 reviews within Etsy policy; a month-by-month 12-month plan to $500/mo net (orders, reviews, hours, ad spend; Q4 calendar with the cutoff from E and a gift-certificate fallback); ops carried from the fulfillment report (test books from Lulu + Prodigi, DPA-or-self-host resolved before any deletion claim ships); and the practical wrapper — marketplace sales-tax handling, entity/insurance norms for solo sellers, FTC-relevant AI-assistance disclosure, and the US Copyright Office human-authorship rule as it touches the "human-crafted" claim and what buyers legally own.

**Blind spots — rule on each in one to three lines; none of the prior reports covered them:**
