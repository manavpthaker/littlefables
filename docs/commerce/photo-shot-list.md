## The organizing principle: prove range, not beauty

First draft of this list leaned on one child — Rosa — across most slots. That's wrong,
and for a specific reason: **the listing claims the art style is built from your taste,
not a dropdown of presets.** Ten images of one girl in one painterly style tells a buyer
the opposite. The photos would contradict the copy.

Second cost: one child narrows who self-identifies. A parent of a blonde three-year-old
boy scrolling ten images of a curly-haired girl doesn't see their kid.

So range is not slot 2. It's the spine of the set. Plan on **four to five different
children** — different ages, skin tones, hair, genders — across **three to four
distinguishable art styles**. That's real production work, but it's the same pipeline,
and it doubles as portfolio for every book after this one.

One honesty constraint: only show styles you can actually reproduce on request.

One emotional constraint: do not use child-alone lifestyle photos. When a real-world shot
contains a child, also show parent, grandparent, or caregiver presence. The product
should feel like a family reading ritual, not an isolated kid with a device.

## The thumbnail — A/B it, don't agonize

Etsy's grid shows **photo 1**, not the video, at roughly 170×170.

Two real candidates, and I don't know which wins:

**A · Family reading, real-world photo.** A child between caregivers with the reader on a
tablet. Risk: art detail is smaller, but the family context is warmer and less eerie.

**B · Four children, four styles, 2×2.** Detail dies at 170px — but four *faces* at 2×2
gives each ~85px, which still reads as a face. Front-loads the differentiator and shows
range in the search grid, where competitors show one generic mock-up.

Ship one, swap after 30 days, same as the title alternates in `etsy-listing.md`. Test
every candidate at 170×170 before committing.

## The photo → illustration question

The most compelling image in this category is a real child's photo beside their
illustrated character. It's also the one with a decision attached.

It needs a real child, which means consent. You've already chosen to keep your own kid
unnamed, and a customer's child is not yours to use without explicit written permission
— a checkbox in the Typeform isn't enough for marketing use.

If you want it: **use a stock or composite child**, and say so in the caption. Your video
already handles the underlying anxiety well — *"we work from it, we don't copy it"* —
so the pair would be reinforcing an existing promise rather than making a new one.

## Featured photos — different job

These live on the **shop About page**, under your story. Etsy's own prompt is *"photos of
your process, workspace, or anything that makes your shop stand out."*

Marketing cards are wrong here. Someone on the About page is asking whether a real person
makes this. Show the making:

- **A character sheet** — one kid, three angles, expressions, before any pages exist
- **Rough → final** — loose sketch beside the finished illustration
- **The printed coloring page** from `email-templates.md` #5, on paper, with crayons
- **Your desk**, if you'll shoot it. The most human asset available and the only one no
  competitor can copy.

Pick 3–4. Process beats polish here.

## Two things to fix

**Page count conflict.** The video says *"Twenty pages."* The listing says page count is
*"matched to the age you tell us."* Both can't be true. Resolve, then add the number to
`AT A GLANCE` — a concrete page count is exactly the fine print a custom buyer wants.

**The end card is already right.** It carries `littlefables.app` *and*
`etsy.com/shop/LittleFablesStories`. That pairing — product domain plus verifiable
marketplace presence — is the anti-phishing artifact the gift certificate needs. Reuse
the exact lockup there.
