# Microschool and Co-op Pilot Outreach

This is discovery outreach, not a mass gifting campaign. The first goal is to learn whether a real group has a shared project or experience worth turning into a story. Do not promise a finished product, request photos, or send a link in the first message unless the recipient asks for one.

## Positioning for the first conversation

Lead with the group’s actual work:

> I’m researching a small pilot for learning communities that turn a real project, place, or season of work into a short story children can read together and take home.

Do not lead with “personalized children’s books,” “AI,” “influencer,” “gift,” or a price. This is a project-documentation and learning-artifact conversation first.

When a link will help, explain the product plainly: Little Fables is a quiet story-reading experience for children—illustrated read-aloud pages in the day, a text-only sleepy mode at night, and narration for listening or reading along. The public site is **https://littlefables.app** and the demo story, **“The Lantern of Round Pond,”** is **https://littlefables.app/sample**. The demo shows the reading experience only; a pilot would be authored from the group’s own place, project, questions, and language.

## Instagram fallback

Use only when no public email or contact form is available. Send one short, non-link message. Do not follow with repeated nudges or ask for a review.

> Hi [Name] — I found [Program] through your [specific project/nature/co-op detail]. I’m researching a small pilot that turns one real group project or season of learning into a short illustrated story for the children and families who lived it. I’m not selling anything yet—could I email you two quick questions if there’s a public address you prefer?

If they reply, move to email. Do not attach a sample or ask for child photos until there is an explicit conversation and a clear consent process.

## Pilot offer after a qualified conversation

Only make this offer after the leader describes a real project and a real use for the story:

> I’d like to propose one tightly scoped pilot: a 12–20-page illustrated reader plus printable version based on [specific project]. The group would approve the story and art before delivery. Children do not need to be named or pictured; the place, questions, discoveries, and shared experience can carry the story. I can offer the first pilot at [pilot terms] in exchange for honest feedback and permission to describe the work publicly only if the group approves.

Do not offer a “free book” to a group before confirming who owns the decision, who gives permission, and what the artifact is for.

## Outreach rules

- Email first whenever a public email exists.
- Personalize the first sentence from the prospect’s own site.
- Ask about a recent behavior or project, not whether they like the idea.
- Do not request child names, faces, or photos in discovery.
- Do not promise educational outcomes, therapeutic benefits, or curriculum alignment without evidence.
- Keep pilot scope fixed: one project, one story, one approval path, one revision round.
- Do not ask for reviews in exchange for a free or discounted pilot.
- Log every reply, referral, objection, and next action in the CSV.
