# Parent guide template

Every custom book ships with a `parent-guide.md` next to `story.json`.
It is the deliverable that turns the book from a personalized keepsake
into a working piece of a family's toolkit. The story plants seeds
during calm; the guide activates them during storms.

**Model to read before drafting yours:** the "Gentle Giant's Secret"
appendix at the bottom of
`content/originals/the_moose_who_knew_about_bigness_formatted.md`.
That is the shape. Yours will be shorter for shorter books; the
sections stay the same.

Required sections below. Missing any section → `pnpm order:publish`
refuses to import.

---

## Section 1 — The tool

One named practice the story teaches. Named. Not a general concept.

Structure:

- **Name it clearly** as a heading — three to six words, memorable.
  "The Gentle Giant's Secret." "The Handoff." "The Wouldn't-Have-Found."
  This becomes the shared family vocabulary.
- **List the steps** — usually three. Each step is one line, an action
  verb, and a sensory anchor. Not "calm down"; "plant your feet like
  roots going into the earth."
- **Say why it works** — one sentence per step. Not a lecture; a
  bracketed note in parent-speak. "The longer exhale activates the
  parasympathetic nervous system." "This interrupts the
  action-without-thinking cycle."

Example (from the Moose):

> **The Gentle Giant's Secret**
>
> 1. **Stand still, like a moose in the meadow.** Plant your feet.
>    Feel roots growing down. Tell your body to wait, just for a
>    moment. *This interrupts the action-without-thinking cycle.*
> 2. **Breathe slow, like the mountain air.** In through the nose
>    for four counts. Out through the mouth for six. *The longer
>    exhale activates the parasympathetic nervous system.*
> 3. **Find the soft place behind your strong.** Hand on heart. Feel
>    for the kindness underneath the bigness. *This reconnects the
>    child to their identity as a kind person, separate from the
>    behavior.*

## Section 2 — Key phrases

Two or three short phrases that become shared family language after
the book has been read several times. These are the phrases the parent
uses out loud in the actual next moment — the shortcut into the shared
world of the story.

Choose phrases that:

- **Name the feeling from the outside** — "the bigness came out",
  "the worry bird is fluttering", "the switch flipped".
- **Point at the tool without a lecture** — "let's find the moose",
  "who has Night Watch tonight?", "how close do you want to be today?"
- **Separate behavior from identity** — "my hands did things my heart
  didn't mean", "you're a kind person having a hard moment".

Format:

> **Key phrases**
>
> - **"[phrase]"** — [one sentence: when to use it, what it does]
> - **"[phrase]"** — [one sentence]
> - **"[phrase]"** — [one sentence]

---

## Section 3 — In the moment

Three to five short scripts the parent can invoke *during* the actual
sticky moment. This is the "it's happening right now, my child is
melting down, what do I say" section.

Rules for the scripts:

- **Under 12 words each.** Parents in the moment have no bandwidth for
  paragraphs.
- **Second-person, spoken.** These are lines to say, not concepts to
  understand.
- **No shame.** Never "you know better than that." Always "let's find
  it together" energy.
- **Reference the book by name** where useful — the book is the shared
  world the family is stepping into.

Example (from the Moose):

> **In the moment**
>
> When the bigness has just come out:
>
> - "I see the bigness came out. Let's find the moose together."
> - "Can you stand still like the moose? I'll do it with you."
> - "Your hands did something your heart didn't mean. Let's breathe
>   like the mountains."
> - "Your kindness is still there. Can you feel it under the bigness?"

---

## Section 4 — Preventive use

Two to three scripts for the moment *before* the sticky moment tends to
happen. The bedtime routine before the meltdown. The 5-minute warning
before leaving the playground. The buckle-in before the doctor's
office.

Same rules as in-the-moment: short, spoken, shame-free.

Example:

> **Preventive use**
>
> When you notice tiredness or a transition approaching:
>
> - "I wonder if the moose can help us get ready for bed."
> - "Let's breathe like the mountains before we go inside."
> - "Can you feel where your kindness is right now? Let's make sure
>   it's ready."

---

## Section 5 — After the storm

Two to three scripts for after the sticky moment has passed. This is
where repair happens, where identity gets restored, where the family
comes back together.

Include:

- **A repair line** the child can adopt — the specific words for
  saying sorry, naming what they did, asking to reconnect.
- **A grace line** the parent uses — permission for the tool to have
  failed this time. "Gentle giants keep practicing."
- **A ritual line** — a small physical act (hug, sit together, share
  a snack) that closes the moment.

Example:

> **After the storm**
>
> - "My bigness came out. My hands did things my heart didn't mean."
>   (A repair sentence the child can borrow.)
> - "We know your heart. Your heart is so kind." (What you say back.)
> - "Gentle giants don't get it right every time. Let's practice
>   together." (Permission for imperfection.)

---

## Optional closing line

One sentence at the end, in italics, that carries the book's spirit.
Not required. When it works, it's what parents put on the fridge.

Examples:

> *The biggest creatures must learn the deepest gentleness.*
> *Who has Night Watch tonight?*
> *You already know what to do.*

---

## Length

For a 3–6 age band book: aim for one printed page (roughly 400–700
words in the guide). Long enough to cover the five sections, short
enough that a parent will actually read it before the next storm.
For 7+, the guide can be longer with more nuanced scripts.

## Voice

The parent guide is written **to the parent, not the child**. Warm,
knowing, non-preachy. Assume the parent is smart and tired. Never
condescend. Never lecture. Say the thing and get out of the way.
