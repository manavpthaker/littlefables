## Why the fire station is the book

The Monday-morning sequence is a complete story arc shot in seven minutes,
and nobody staged it:

1. `IMG_8819` — the boys at the threshold, Azi turned to look at Averill.
2. `IMG_8830` / `IMG_8842` — standing on the apron, looking up. Averill has
   a tiny toy car clenched in his fist. He keeps it there the whole time.
3. `IMG_8848` — Averill planted and still; Azi doing a big stompy dance
   step, watching Averill for a reaction.
4. **`IMG_8825` — the two of them from behind, hands joined, facing into
   the bay.** This is the emotional centre of the whole set and the anchor
   image for page 8.
5. `IMG_8852` / `IMG_8856` — inside the cab among the helmets and red bags,
   red dome lights overhead, Averill upright in a space built for adults.

That is a textbook gradual-exposure ladder — watch from safety, watch
closer, small chosen approach, arrival — which is why the book runs on
`story-patterns.md` §7 rather than as a travelogue. The pattern was already
in the photographs.

## The relationship

Azi is roughly a year older than Averill — about three and a half to
Averill's two and a half at the time of the trip. **One year, not two.** They
are near-peers, which is the whole point: the one who goes first is barely
ahead. Across the fire-station set Azi moves first and then turns back to
check — at the threshold, on the apron, in the cab. Averill stands still,
holds his little car, and comes when Azi looks back. That small asymmetry is
the book's engine and is why the tool has two people in it.

## What Averill is into (from the family, 2026-08-14)

Movies, wrestling, **monster trucks**, tap-tap phone games, the zoo, the
splashpad, the indoor playground. **Obsessed with *Cars*.** Liked *Planes*.
Liked *The Lion King*. Not much screen time — occasionally a movie night.
Direct quote: "Dude monster trucks rules."

Two of these earned their way into the story:

- **Monster trucks** are his unit of bigness, so page 4 measures the fire
  engine against one. That is his own vocabulary, not an adult's.
- **Cars** is why the little die-cast in his fist carries the whole spine.
  It turns out to be a **police car** (crop of `IMG_8842` — "POLICE" legible
  on the door), which makes the book a small emergency vehicle walking into
  a building full of enormous ones. That rhyme was already in the photos;
  the interest just confirms why he never put it down.

The rest — zoo, splashpad, indoor playground, wrestling, movie night — were
deliberately left out of the story text and put in `parent-guide.md` §7 as
places to use the tool instead. The doctrine opens by naming the failure
mode as "a highlight reel of the child's interests dressed as a story", and
five more interests crammed into 192 words is exactly that.

## The place, from the Airbnb listing

[rooms/28589796](https://www.airbnb.com/rooms/28589796), read 2026-08-14.

- "Luxury Secluded Home on Equestrian Estate" — a farm stay
- On the **96-acre estate of the Connecticut Equestrian Center**
- Overlooks the **Skungamaug river**; river trails and country walks
- Horse riding lessons by arrangement; two squash courts in the barn
- 7 bedrooms, 11 beds, 4 baths, 16+ guests — hence "so many doors" on page 2
- 15 minutes from UConn Storrs
- Host Richard, Superhost, 4.93 over 364 reviews

**Now used.** The family confirmed (2026-08-14) that they walked along the
creek and explored the farm and the horses. That unlocked the Skungamaug,
which was the best word in the whole setting and which I had parked as
unusable while it was unverified.

It earns its place twice over:

- It is a genuinely delightful thing for a three-year-old to say, and it is
  the book's word-tap moment (`Skun-ga-maug`).
- More importantly, the creek gave the story somewhere to **rehearse the
  refrain**. Azi hops a stepping stone and says "one step, then you" where
  the stakes are a wet sock; the identical line then lands at the fire
  station where the stakes are real. The first draft had the phrase appear
  once, cold, and it had to carry the whole book alone. It no longer does.

There are no photographs of the creek walk, so pages 3 and 4 are written
from the family's account and kept deliberately generic — shallow New
England woodland creek, no identifiable detail invented.
