# Character notes — One Step, Then Me

Use these verbatim for recurring character descriptions in every page prompt.
Both children are **real humans**, drawn as modern real children in
contemporary everyday clothes. Never plush, never animal-like, never
costumed.

---

**Averill:** small human boy, about two and a half at the time of the trip —
a little younger and rounder than Azi, and close to him in size. Fair skin
with pink cheeks, fine straight ash-blond hair cut in a soft chin-length bob
with a full fringe across the forehead, pale blue-grey eyes, round toddler
face, small chin. Solid, slightly bow-legged toddler stance; stands very
still when he is taking something in. Almost always holding **the little
police car** in one closed fist — a small die-cast black-and-white police
cruiser with chunky black wheels, white door panels and a low light bar;
scuffed from being carried everywhere. It is the same object on every page
and it must stay consistent. Grey short-sleeved t-shirt with a small
woodland-and-sun graphic on the chest, dark trousers, black low-top sneakers
with white soles. He is the one who watches first and moves second.

**Azi (Azad):** human boy about three and a half at the time of the trip —
roughly a year older than Averill and a little taller and leaner, with warm
medium-brown skin and dense tousled black curls forming a rounded halo with
ringlets across his forehead and around his ears. Large dark expressive eyes,
soft round cheeks. Beige-and-white fine gingham short-sleeve shirt buttoned
up, with a small band collar and sleeves pushed to the elbow; brown corduroy
trousers; black sneakers with white soles and a yellow flash. Physically
expressive — dances, stomps, lifts a knee, throws his whole body into a
moment — and repeatedly turns to check where Averill is. He is the one who
goes first and looks back.

**The relationship, which is the whole book:** they are close in age — about
a year apart — so this is not a big kid towing a baby. Azi is only slightly
bigger, and both of them are small next to everything else. That is what
makes the leading count: the one going first is barely ahead. He leads by a
step and a half, never more. In group scenes the two of them are a unit
slightly apart from the adults. When they are amazed or unsure, they end up
shoulder to shoulder, and often hand in hand.

**Draw them as near-peers.** Roughly a head's difference at most. If the art
reads as "toddler and big kid", the gap is too wide.

## Setting anchors

**The fire station:** a small-town municipal engine bay, concrete apron out
front, one enormous roll-up door standing wide open with white painted
jamb. Inside, two engines nose-out in the bays — lime-yellow over white
bodies, chrome, red-and-white chevron striping on the rear. Coiled bright
yellow exhaust-extraction hoses hang in long catenary curves from the
ceiling. Yellow-and-black striped safety line along the ceiling track.
Grey painted floor with a yellow lane stripe. A third vehicle sits deep in
the back of the bay, visible through to the far door.

**The engine cab interior:** dim grey-green, close and cave-like. A row of
red and clear dome lights set into the ceiling. High black seats far too
big for a small child. Coiled radio cables, hanging harnesses, white
helmets racked at the side, red equipment bags. Daylight comes through the
windows in one bright band.

**The paddock:** post-and-rail wooden fence, mown green field running
downhill, two horses grazing at middle distance — one chestnut bay, one
pale. Heavy deciduous woods behind, full late-summer green. A long
cream-sided barn along the right edge with a mown lawn beside it.

**The house:** a large pale-walled family room, dark leather sofa, big
patterned grey-and-cream area rug, blond hardwood floor, glass-paned
interior doors, tall windows with green trees outside. The building toy is
a clear plastic box of bright interlocking gears and rods in yellow,
orange, red and purple.

---

## Style anchor

Warm children's picture-book watercolor illustration, soft edges,
hand-painted feel, cozy palette (paper cream #F2E8D2, warm amber, ochre,
brick red, sage green, late-summer green), one clear focal moment, natural
composition, gentle depth, no text, no logos, no watermarks, no
photorealism, no harsh contrast.

**Scale is the subject of this book.** In every fire-station page the
children must read as genuinely small against the machine — low camera,
child eye-level, the engine cropped so it runs out of the frame. The point
is never lost by making the truck cute or toy-sized.
