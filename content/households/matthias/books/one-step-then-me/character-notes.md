# Character notes — One Step, Then Me

## Cast rule — paste verbatim into every prompt

The only real humans in this book are Averill and Azi. Both were supplied by
the family. Do not invent additional human faces. Background figures, when
unavoidable, must be distant and faceless.

Both boys are modern real children in contemporary everyday clothes. Never
draw them as plush, animal-like, costumed, or photorealistic.

## Canonical character blocks — paste verbatim

The reference photographs were taken about one year before illustration.
Use them for identity, hair, skin tone, and relationship only—**do not copy
their photographed toddler body proportions**. Depict both boys visibly one
year older, with longer preschooler limbs, higher waists, smaller head-to-body
ratios, steadier posture, and less baby-round silhouettes. Preserve their
one-year age gap.

**Averill:** Three-and-a-half-year-old preschool boy with fair pink-cheeked
skin, pale blue-grey eyes, ash-blond bob and fringe, longer limbs, lean build,
grey T-shirt, dark trousers, black sneakers.

**Azi:** Four-and-a-half-year-old preschool boy with warm medium-brown skin,
large dark almond eyes, forward-falling black curls, longer limbs, coordinated
build, beige gingham shirt, brown corduroys, black sneakers.

**Police car:** Small scuffed die-cast black-and-white police cruiser with
chunky black wheels, white door panels, and a low light bar.

## Relationship and action direction

They are near-peers, about one year apart. Azi is only slightly taller—never
more than one head. He moves first and looks back; Averill watches first and
moves second. In uncertain moments they end shoulder to shoulder or hand in
hand. Do not stage this as a big child towing a baby.

Azi likeness lock: use `reference/IMG_8833.HEIC`, `reference/IMG_8834.HEIC`,
and `reference/IMG_8830.HEIC` for his face and hair. His curls are uneven and
fall forward around his forehead and ears, not a neat round afro or generic
spiky cartoon hair. Keep his large dark eyes, warm medium-brown skin, soft
rounded cheeks, small mouth, beige gingham shirt, and brown corduroys. Do not
copy adult hands or any readable vehicle markings from the reference photos.

## Setting anchors

**The fire station:** a small-town municipal engine bay, concrete apron out
front, one enormous roll-up door standing wide open with white painted
jamb. Inside, two engines nose-out in the bays — lime-yellow over white
bodies, chrome, red-and-white chevron striping on the rear. Coiled bright
yellow exhaust-extraction hoses hang in long catenary curves from the
ceiling. Yellow-and-black striped safety line along the ceiling track.
Grey painted floor with a yellow lane stripe. A third vehicle sits deep in
the back of the bay, visible through to the far door.

**The engine cab interior:** dim grey-green, close and cave-like. A row of
red and clear dome lights set into the ceiling. High black seats far too
big for a small child. Coiled radio cables, hanging harnesses, white
helmets racked at the side, red equipment bags. Daylight comes through the
windows in one bright band.

**The paddock:** post-and-rail wooden fence, mown green field running
downhill, two horses grazing at middle distance — one chestnut bay, one
pale. Heavy deciduous woods behind, full late-summer green. A long
cream-sided barn along the right edge with a mown lawn beside it.

**The house:** a large pale-walled family room, dark leather sofa, big
patterned grey-and-cream area rug, blond hardwood floor, glass-paned
interior doors, tall windows with green trees outside. The building toy is
a clear plastic box of bright interlocking gears and rods in yellow,
orange, red and purple.

## Approved style anchor — paste verbatim into every prompt

Handmade torn-and-cut construction-paper collage, every object assembled from
flat matte paper pieces with deckled fibrous edges, visible paper grain, slight
warping, tiny gaps, and shallow glue-lift shadows between layers; scissor-cut
simple geometry mixed with torn organic shapes; sparse colored-pencil marks only
for faces, shoe seams, gingham lines, and tiny prop details; limited cream,
forest green, terracotta, mustard, smoky teal, warm brown, black, white, and
bright lime-yellow palette; playful monumental scale for ages 3-6;
unmistakably paper, not painting; no airbrushed shading, no watercolor wash, no
smooth digital gradients, no glossy 3D clay, felt, foam, or plastic look, no
photorealism, no visible text, readable letters, logos, watermarks, UI, or
unnecessary screens, no modern tech unless the story requires it.

Approved from preview **Style B — Cut-paper adventure** on 2026-08-14; tightened
for a full-art redo on 2026-08-15 after family feedback that the first pass did
not read as paper enough and Azi did not yet look enough like Azi. The
source of truth is `previews/APPROVED-prompt.txt`; do not reconstruct or
paraphrase it.

**Scale is the subject of this book.** In every fire-station image the
children must read as genuinely small against the machine—low camera,
child-eye level, engine cropped beyond the frame. Never make the engine cute
or toy-sized.
