# Setting and source material — *The Fort in Coventry*

## What this book is

Imagined based on the real Labor Day weekend in Coventry, Connecticut
(Aug 29–31, 2025). Second matthias book. The fort is invented; every
place it opens onto is real, drawn from the same photo run and family
account as `../one-step-then-me/`. The premise is portal-adventure at the
4–6 band: the boys build a fort in the too-big Airbnb, and each time they
crawl out the fort's other side they land somewhere else on the farm —
but bigger, brighter, weirder, at child-height scale.

The previous book (one-step-then-me) tried §7 (fear of a specific thing)
adapted to awe, and the family read it as a travelogue with a refrain
glued on. Accurate: the pattern had been softened until Averill never
actually needed the tool. This book goes celebration/witnessing per the
doctrine's "when in doubt" section, at the buyer's explicit request for
an adventure. The tool ("The Fort Radio") is real and borrowable; the
mission is real (find the sleeping giant); every real weekend detail is
a plot beat, not a page of scenery.

## What earned its way in

Every real weekend detail was auditioned against the same test: does it
*move the plot*, or is it decoration? Decoration was cut.

- **The big house's "so many doors"** (7BR / 11 beds / 16+ guests per the
  Airbnb listing) is the counting hook on page 1, and the reason a small
  nook exists on page 2 for the fort to be built in.
- **The die-cast police car** (identified from a crop of `IMG_8842` —
  "POLICE" legible on the door in the original) is the *mission-giving
  radio*, not a talisman in Averill's fist. It crackles on pages 5 and 10
  and BLINKs on page 13. This is the biggest reframe from the previous
  book, where the car was a prop; here it is a character.
- **Skungamaug** (the actual creek, called out on the Airbnb listing —
  overlooks the Skungamaug river) is the word-tap moment on page 7 and
  the first "no giant here" beat. Same phonetic guide as the previous
  book (`Skun-ga-maug`) — see `story.json` pronunciations.
- **The horses over the paddock fence** — real (family confirmed) — become
  the second "no giant here" beat on page 9, reimagined at unnerving
  preschooler-eye scale (noses like doors, teeth long as a hand, hooves
  in the ribs). Real horses are two: one chestnut bay, one pale.
- **The fire station** — real (`IMG_8819`–`8858`) — is the climax on
  pages 11–12. It is the mission's answer: the sleeping giant. Reached
  from a mission the child was given, not stumbled into.

## Deliberately left out

- **The wrestling / movie night / zoo / splashpad / indoor playground /
  monster trucks / *Cars* obsession** — Averill's stated interests from
  the family (2026-08-14). Monster trucks earns one line ("bigger than a
  monster truck") on page 12 as his own unit of bigness. *Cars* is why
  the die-cast is a character rather than a prop. The rest stay in
  `parent-guide.md` §Real-world rungs as places to use the tool. Same
  reason as the last book: the doctrine's opening failure mode is "a
  highlight reel of the child's interests dressed as a story."
- **The Airbnb's hero shot** (peak autumn foliage on the listing) is not
  what this trip looked like. Full green everywhere. Do not let autumn
  foliage into the art.
- **Adults, other children, the other families in the paddock-fence
  photos** — cast rule holds. Only Averill and Azi in the art. No
  invented humans, no distant faceless figures on the plot pages either;
  the mythic pages are deserted-farm quiet on purpose.

## What was *added* that isn't in the photos

Only three inventions, all called out here so future edits can trace
them:

1. **The fort itself.** No fort was built on the real trip. It is the
   premise. The nook it is built in is imagined from the big house's
   general geometry (staircase alcove, bookcase recess, or bay-window
   corner — art director's pick).
2. **The police car's radio.** Real toy, imagined function. The doctrine
   allows non-human voices (§3.1) and this is an object voice, which
   avoids the fabricated-humans rule.
3. **The "sleeping giant" framing of the fire engine.** The engine was
   awake and the boys went inside the cab in real life. Here it sleeps —
   which is what unlocks the ending's restraint beat ("should we wake
   it?" "no, not today") and keeps the world intact for the parent to
   re-invoke on the next new-place threshold.

## The relationship

Same as the previous book: Azi is roughly a year older than Averill,
they are near-peers (one year, not two), Azi moves first and looks back,
Averill watches first and moves second. **New in this book:** on page 6
that dynamic is stated explicitly ("Azi went first, like always. Averill
went second, like always"), and on page 11 it is *reversed* — Averill
goes first because the police car is on the mission and the mission is
his. The reversal is the emotional beat of the book. The illustration
must carry it (see `character-notes.md` § Relationship and action
direction).
