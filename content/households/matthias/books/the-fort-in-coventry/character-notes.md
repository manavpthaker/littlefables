# Character notes — The Fort in Coventry

## Cast rule — paste verbatim into every prompt

The only real humans in this book are Averill and Azi. Both were supplied by
the family. Do not invent additional human faces. Background figures, when
unavoidable, must be distant and faceless.

Both boys are modern real children in contemporary everyday clothes. Never
draw them as plush, animal-like, costumed, or photorealistic.

## Illustration-age override, confirmed 2026-08-15

**This book runs one year older than the previous matthias book.** The 4–6
band means Averill is drawn as about four and a half, and Azi as about five
and a half. Reference photos are about a year older than that shift on top of
what they already were — so add roughly two years of preschooler-age growth
onto the reference images: even longer limbs, higher waists, smaller
head-to-body ratios, steadier posture, less baby-round silhouettes. Preserve
the one-year age gap. **Do not make Azi look like an older sibling; they are
still near-peers.**

## Canonical character blocks — paste verbatim

**Averill:** Four-and-a-half-year-old preschool boy with fair pink-cheeked
skin, pale blue-grey eyes, ash-blond bob and fringe just beginning to grow
past his ears, longer limbs, lean build, grey T-shirt, dark trousers, black
sneakers. Almost always holding a small die-cast police car in his fist.

**Azi:** Five-and-a-half-year-old preschool boy with warm medium-brown skin,
large dark almond eyes, forward-falling black curls, longer limbs,
coordinated build, beige gingham shirt, brown corduroys, black sneakers.

**The police car:** Small scuffed die-cast black-and-white police cruiser
with chunky black wheels, white door panels, a low light bar, and (in this
book only, when the story calls for it) a tiny warm-glowing light on the
roof that goes BLINK on the reveal page. It is a real toy in Averill's
hand; the glow is a "children's-book truth" convention, drawn as a soft
watercolor bloom, not as a special effect.

## Relationship and action direction

They are near-peers, about one year apart. Azi is only slightly taller —
never more than one head. **Book-specific dynamic:** Azi always goes first
through the fort's other side. Averill always goes second. This is
established explicitly on page 6 ("Azi went first, like always. Averill went
second, like always") and reversed on page 11, where Averill goes first
because he is the one carrying the police car and the police car is on the
mission. The reversal is the emotional beat of the book — the illustration
must sell it. On page 11 Averill's posture, stride, and gaze are the ones
that lead; Azi is one step behind, watching him with a small look of "oh —
you're doing it."

## Setting anchors (see setting-notes.md for full source material)

The book uses four locations, each shown twice — once as the "real"
weekend detail and once as the mythic portal version reached through the
fort's other side. The mythic versions are the same place, from a
child-height camera, with everything one or two notches bigger and more
dreamlike than in life. The reference photos anchor the real geometry; the
mythic exaggeration is in the scale, the light, and the paint.

**The big house — real:** a large pale-walled family room, dark leather
sofa, big patterned grey-and-cream area rug, blond hardwood floor,
glass-paned interior doors, tall windows with green trees outside. Twelve
doors visible (or countable) in the establishing spread on page 1.

**The nook:** an underused corner of the big house — under a staircase, or
in the recess between two bookcases, or the deep alcove of a bay window.
Just tall enough for a preschooler to stand upright. This is where the fort
gets built.

**The fort (interior view):** blanket-and-cushion tent, warm dark, one
narrow slot of daylight leaking in from the real house. Airborne dust
motes in the shaft of light. This is the transitional space between the
real and the mythic; it must feel *safe* and slightly magic — not scary,
not cold.

**The creek — mythic:** shallow New England woodland creek reimagined at
half-scale-up. Reeds taller than a preschooler. Rocks the size of shoes to
step on. Dappled light through overhead leaves. No identifiable landmark
invented; the creek is a place, not a picture of a place. Colors: forest
green, wet slate, dark umber, water-silver.

**The horse field — mythic:** post-and-rail wooden fence, mown field, two
horses drawn at unnerving preschooler-eye scale — noses coming down toward
the reader like the top of a door swinging shut, teeth long as a hand,
hooves large enough to fill the lower quarter of the page. **Not scary —
awed.** The horses do not mind the boys at all. One chestnut bay, one pale.

**The fire station — mythic:** the wide-wide-place at the climax. Concrete
apron in the foreground (page 11), one enormous roll-up door standing wide
open, white painted jamb. On page 12 we are inside the bay looking at the
engine at rest — lime-yellow-over-white body (or classic red if the
composition needs it; the story text says "red as a fire" so lean red for
this book), chrome accents, cavernous ceiling, coiled bright yellow
exhaust-extraction hoses hanging in long catenary curves. **The engine is
"asleep"** — the illustration should read as a sleeping beast: closed
headlights, quiet posture, no crew visible, no other trucks in the bay.
Bigger than a monster truck, bigger than a whale, bigger than anything —
so cropped in-frame that the reader cannot see all of it.

## Approved style anchor — paste verbatim into every prompt

**Sendak-style ink and watercolor** — crosshatched pen line on cream stock,
transparent watercolor washes over the ink, visible paper texture through
the paint, brushwork rough at the edges of shapes, ink pooling in the
crosshatch, small halos of raw paper left where highlights land. Palette
built around forest green, dusk blue, warm cream, deep umber, terracotta,
and one saturated fire-engine red used sparingly across the book and full-
tilt on page 12 for the sleeping giant. Line weight varies with mood:
tight and fine for the house, looser and shakier at the mythic-portal
pages, densest crosshatch inside the fort where it is warm and dark. Read
aloud like *Where the Wild Things Are* and *In the Night Kitchen*: kids on
adventure, real objects made monumental at child height, dream logic
without menace. No airbrushed shading, no smooth digital gradients, no
glossy 3D clay/felt/plastic look, no photorealism, no visible text,
readable letters, logos, watermarks, UI, or unnecessary screens.

Approved 2026-08-15 as the fresh direction for this book. The source of
truth is `previews/APPROVED-prompt.txt`; do not reconstruct or paraphrase
it.

**Scale is still the subject on the climax pages.** In every fire-station
image (pages 11 and 12) the children must read as genuinely small against
the machine — low camera, child-eye level, engine cropped beyond the
frame. Never make the engine cute or toy-sized. This is where the book's
whole promise lands.
