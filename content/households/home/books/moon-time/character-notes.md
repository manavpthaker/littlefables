Azi/Azad: preschool-age human boy with Colombian and Indian family heritage, warm medium-brown skin, soft round cheeks, large dark expressive eyes, and dense tousled black curls forming a rounded halo with ringlets across his forehead and around his ears. Small red thread bracelet. Curious, thoughtful, playful; often leaning close, sitting on the floor, barefoot, with a guitar, keyboard, puzzle, or family nearby. Modern everyday child clothes only; never plush, animal-like, furry, monkey-like, muzzle-faced, or dressed in ethnic/traditional clothing unless the page text explicitly calls for it. In this book he wears soft blue rocket pajamas throughout, and in the moon sequence a simple white play spacesuit worn over them, with three small round warm lights in a row on the chest.

The moon: not a cartoon face and never anthropomorphized — it is the actual moon, rendered enormous and close, filling the bedroom window edge to edge. Grey and gold and ancient, its whole surface covered in craters drawn as circles inside circles and rings inside rings, like a pond frozen mid-splash. Its presence is warm rather than eerie. It never has eyes or a mouth. Its speech is shown through light and composition, never through facial features.

Indira/Mama: modern human mother in her 40s, Colombian by heritage, with warm medium-brown skin, dark almond eyes, expressive arched brows, dark hair neatly pulled back at home or smooth shoulder-length on outings, warm playful smile, delicate gold jewelry, and sometimes thin round gold glasses. Contemporary everyday clothes, never ethnic/traditional/costume clothing unless the page text explicitly calls for it. She feels gentle, attentive, and physically close to Azi, often crouched, leaning in, or down at his eye level. Never plush, animal-like, or generic. In this book she wears soft modern at-home evening clothes in dusty rose and oatmeal.

Manav/Papa/Pap: modern human father in his 40s, Indian/Gujarati by heritage, with warm medium-brown skin, short dark hair with close faded sides, neatly trimmed full beard with subtle salt-and-pepper, dark kind eyes behind round glasses or clear round frames, expressive brows, and a broad playful grin. Contemporary everyday clothes such as tan jacket, brown overshirt, denim shirt, or white tee; never ethnic/traditional/costume clothing unless the page text explicitly calls for it. He is steady and reachable, often leaning or sitting close to Azi. Never plush, animal-like, or clean-shaven unless specifically requested. In this book he wears a worn navy henley and soft grey joggers.

The companions: soft, well-loved plush toys with visible seams and slightly matted fur, never realistic animals and never cartoon-slick. Jujy is a black-and-white tuxedo cat, confident posture, chest out. Dory is a soft grey-and-white cat with a dreamy unfocused gaze. Pandies is a worn panda, older and more faded than the rest, calm and settled. Clappy is red and round with short arms held slightly out. Monkie wears footie pajamas with too many pockets. Slothie is long-limbed and drowsy with Baby Slothie clinging to one arm. In the moon sequence they appear at scale beside Azi, still plush, never transformed into real creatures.

The rocket: a large cardboard appliance box stood on end, with a rectangular door cut into one side, forty-one hand-drawn dials in marker across the interior wall, and no nose cone. From Part Two onward the left side is visibly crumpled inward. From Part Eight onward it is taped from the inside, so the tape does not show — but the dent remains.

The rock: small, grey, irregular, with a faint mica sparkle when turned. Appears in Azi's fist from Part Seven, and on the playroom shelf beside the record player from Part Eight.

Style addendum for this book: warm nocturnal storybook illustration, deep indigo and ink-blue night interiors lit by a single lamp or by moonlight through glass, hand-painted gouache with soft grain, generous negative space, moon-silver and pale gold highlights against dusty warm neutrals. Interiors are cozy and lived-in, never tidy. The moon sequence shifts to a paler, cooler, dustier palette — soft greys, chalk, and faint violet shadow — while keeping the same paint texture, so it reads as the same book rather than a different world. No lens flare, no digital glow, no printed captions, no borders, no text.
