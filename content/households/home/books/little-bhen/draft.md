# Azi's Little Bhen

**Format:** Board book · **Age band:** 0–3 as requested *(see production note — this reads 2–5)*
**Length:** 15 reader pages · 325 words
**Meter:** Rhyming couplets, four beats a line — *Little Blue Truck* cadence
**Refrain:** *"Into the box it goes, it goes — / for Kaira, Kaira, Kaira."* (×5, once per gift)
**Heritage words:** *bhen* (sister) · *ladoo* (sweet) · *mira* (look) · Kaka, Kaki, Dada, Dadi
**Premise:** Kaka and Kaki have a baby girl — Kaira — in Los Angeles. Azi's bhen. The whole family gets ready to meet her, one gift each.
**Skills seeded:** Waiting and anticipation · Giving · Family as a system · Naming a new relationship

## Backmatter

**Words to know**

**bhen** *(BEN)* — sister, in Gujarati.

> In Gujarati families there is no separate word for *cousin.* Kaka's daughter isn't Azi's cousin. She's his sister. That's the whole book.

**ladoo** *(LAA-doo)* — a round Indian sweet, about the size of a plum.

**mira** *(MEE-rah)* — *look,* in Spanish. Mama's word.

**Kaka** *(KAH-kah)* — father's younger brother. **Kaki** *(KAH-kee)* — Kaka's wife.

**Dada** *(DAH-dah)* — father's father. **Dadi** *(DAH-dee)* — father's mother.

**For the grown-up reading along**

The refrain is the melody. Let it drop a note lower each time, and by the third round let the child say *"Kaira, Kaira, Kaira"* with you. That's the song.

Page 14 is the one to slow down for. Stop. Let them look. It doesn't need to be read fast.

## Production notes

**1. Age band.** You asked for 0–3, and for *Little Blue Truck* rhymes. Those pull against each other. The Azi-Verse spec puts board books at 0–100 words; *Little Blue Truck* is ~230 words and is published 2–5. This is 325. It's a **2–5 picture book** by every measure except the format you asked for. Two options: band it 2–5, or I cut ~200 words for a true board book. The verse survives the cut — the four gift beats are the spine, everything else is trim.

**2. Page count won't bind.** Board books come in multiples of 4 (12 / 16 / 20 / 24). 15 pages is a printer conversation. Adding a title page and an endpaper gets you to 16.

**3. The spelling of *bhen*.** Worth a decision before print. Gujarati બહેન is usually romanized **ben** or **behen** — the *bh* digraph marks the aspirated consonant in *bhai* (brother), which is why the honorific suffix is *-ben* (Anandi**ben**) and not *-bhen*. Separately and more practically: *"bhen"* on its own is the first word of a common Hindi expletive, which is a real risk on a cover. Every rhyme in the book (*when*, *then*, *ten*) works identically with **ben**. It's your family's word and your title, so I left it as you wrote it — but it's a one-command swap if you want it.

**4. Kaka & Kaki in Los Angeles is new canon.** The Universe Guide doesn't place them. Worth adding so the next story doesn't contradict it. Also note *Kaka* specifically means father's *younger* brother — the guide describes Kaka's house as where "Papa becomes someone's little brother again," which would make Kaka the elder. One of the two needs to give.

**5. Colombian balance.** Mama's "*Mira!*" on page 6 is the only Spanish in the book. Enough to keep it from reading as fully Gujarati-coded, but this is a Kaka-side story and it leans that way by design.
