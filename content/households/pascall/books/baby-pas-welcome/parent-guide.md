# For the grown-ups — how to use *Welcome, Baby Pas*

This book is a lullaby *and* a small toolkit for you — for the two of
you, in the first year of being her people. The story is what you'll
read aloud in the rocking chair; the sections below are what to reach
for when the rocking chair feels like the whole world.

Read this once when the book arrives. Then keep it somewhere near where
you feed her at 3am. The story plants the seeds during calm; the guide
is what those seeds grow into when you are tired.

---

## The tool: The Wobbly Step

The book teaches one thing to you, softly: **your love has been in
motion long before it was witnessed.** Page 3 is the anchor — Papa
fixed the wobbly step, tuned the little lamp, before Baby Pas needed
any of it. You have been doing that too. You are doing it right now,
even in the moments that don't feel like it.

When the newborn haze makes you doubt yourself, this is the three-step
reframe. Say it to each other. Say it to yourself.

1. **Name what you already did.**
   Out loud. "I folded the blanket. I set the lamp. I sang the songs
   no one heard yet." *This works because early-parent love is
   invisible to the child it's for. Naming it makes it real again to
   you.*

2. **Look at one small thing that is working.**
   The window is open. The room is warm. The blanket is where it
   should be. *This interrupts the "I am failing at everything" loop.
   You are not. You are running the whole house while learning a
   person.*

3. **Say her name.**
   Baby Pas. Out loud. She is the reason. *Speaking her name puts
   the exhaustion in context — not "I can't do this" but "I am
   learning her." The learning is the doing.*

## Key phrases

Two lines from the book that become shared vocabulary between you
after a few reads:

- **"She is new, and she is so wanted."** — Say this when one of you
  is spiralling. It is the whole book in nine words. The wanting is
  the anchor; the new-ness is just the season you are in.
- **"The whole house says so."** — When the practical logistics of a
  newborn make you feel small (the pumping, the laundry, the pediatric
  paperwork), remember that the house you have built together is
  saying welcome on your behalf, even when your voice is tired.

---

## In the moment — when it's hard right now

Three lines under twelve words each, for the 3am moment, the "she
won't settle" moment, the "I don't know what I'm doing" moment. Pick
one. Don't try all three.

- **"I fixed the wobbly step. I know how to do small things."**
  (For when the big things feel impossible. You have already done
  many small things. This one is small too.)
- **"She is new. So am I."**
  (For when you feel like you should already know. Neither of you
  has been here before. That is the whole assignment.)
- **"The house says so."**
  (Two words. For when you have no bandwidth for a full sentence.
  Look at the room. It is holding her. It is holding you too.)

If it's still hard after all three — that's not failure. Hand her
to your partner if you can. Put her in the crib and step to the
window for one minute. See "after the storm" below.

---

## Preventive — before the hard hour

Two scripts for the calm moment right before the hard one. The five
minutes before the evening witching-hour, the settle before the
overnight shift, the pause before your partner leaves for work.

- **"Let's read Baby Pas her book."** — Read it aloud to her when
  she's calm. She won't parse the words for months. You will. The
  reading is a reminder for you.
- **"Which page do you want to be in tonight?"** — A shared bedtime
  ritual between the two of you. Pick a page from the book that
  matches how you want the night to feel. Some nights are the
  wobbly-step night. Some are the "here you are" night.

---

## After the storm — after a hard night

Three lines for the morning after. Sunlight, cold coffee, everyone
still alive.

- **"That was a night. I was there."** *(A repair line for
  yourself — you don't need to have been perfect, only present.)*
- **"The birds sang the ordinary songs, which are the best songs."**
  *(Line from page 5. Say it looking out the window while the
  kettle boils. Some mornings that is enough.)*
- **"We are learning her. We are allowed to be new."** *(A grace
  line between the two of you. Neither of you is failing. You are
  both learning.)*

Do not compare your night to anyone else's. Do not scroll for other
parents doing it better. Read page 8. The whole house says so. The
whole world says so.

---

## For the giver

Uncle Manav — this book is more than a gift; the whole thing is
written in your voice. When they open it at the shower, or when they
read it aloud in the rocking chair later, they are hearing what you
wanted her to know about them before she was even here.

A few notes for you specifically:

- **Read it first, out loud, to yourself.** Once, alone. The book
  won't sound the way you expect until you hear it in your own voice.
  Small things you will want to change will surface. Change them.
- **Read it to them at the shower.** If you can bear it — you'll be
  the first voice she hears these words in through her mama's belly,
  and her mama and papa will be the second and third. That is a
  small ceremony worth having.
- **When she is old enough to hear it as her own book** (four? five?),
  read it to her yourself. She will ask who Azad is. She will find
  out that her papa was already an uncle before he was a papa. She
  will find out that the whole time she was on her way, she was being
  written about with love. That's the second reading, and it's the
  one that lasts.

*You are new to this world, Baby Pas. And you are being handed to two
of the best people I know.*
