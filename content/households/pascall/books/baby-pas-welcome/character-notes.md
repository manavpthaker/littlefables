# Welcome, Baby Pas — Character Notes

## Cast rule (READ FIRST)

**Baby Pas is not yet born.** There is no photo of her — the reference at
`reference/parents-photo.jpg` is a photo of her **parents-to-be**. She
cannot be given invented features. Depict her only as:

- a small bundle in a blanket (page 6, page 8),
- a shape in Mama's hands (page 6),
- a small head against Papa's shoulder (page 7),
- suggested by absence in the earlier pages (an empty crib being prepared,
  a folded blanket, a hummed song).

**Never a face-forward portrait. Never open eyes rendered in detail.** If
her cheek or an ear peeks out of the blanket in the final page, it is soft
and unspecified — the illustration equivalent of a newborn photo taken
from behind. The buyer's friends' actual child will grow into her own face;
we do not preempt it.

**Baby Pas is a girl** (per intake — feminine pronouns throughout).
Name: "Pas," pronounced like "pass." It is Uncle Manav's affectionate
placeholder — the parents haven't chosen the real name yet, and that is
part of what makes the book tender. Do not add any surname or nickname
in the art.

## Mama and Papa

The reference photo shows both parents together. The likeness is a *guide,
not a portrait*: soft, painterly, from-life-but-not-identifying. Think of
how Emily Winfield Martin or Tabitha Paige render adults — recognizable to
the family, but stylized enough that they read as archetypal parents in
their own children's book.

- **Mama** — reading from the updated private references: warm brown skin,
  long neat dark braids, bright smile, softly rounded cheeks, and gold hoop
  earrings. Draw her in soft everyday clothes (a knit cardigan in cream or
  moss green, a long dress or comfortable pants). No maternity uniform — by
  the book's coda she is holding the baby, so the pregnancy is off-page. Her
  hands are the recurring anchor: capable, warm, learning.
- **Papa** — reading from the updated private references: medium-deep brown
  skin, close-cropped dark hair with a tidy hairline, dark expressive eyes, a
  trimmed mustache and goatee, and a lean gentle face. Draw him in a soft
  henley or button-down, sleeves rolled. His hands work — page 3 he is fixing
  a small thing. His shoulder is the anchor on page 7: broad enough for a
  small head, gentle.

Both parents' faces can be shown, but softly — three-quarter angles, warm
downcast eyes on the baby in the coda, painterly not photographic. The
book is not a portrait commission; it is a welcome.

## Style anchor — Tabitha Paige-inspired watercolor

The buyer named **Tabitha Paige** as the inspiration. Her signature:
loose watercolor washes, botanical detail (ferns, wildflowers, moss),
warm cream paper, soft naturalistic palette (moss green, sage, dusty
peach, terracotta, warm cream, hazy dusk-blue), a hand-drawn quality
where the pencil sketch shows through the paint, unfussy compositions
with lots of paper showing.

Full style anchor for the preview covers (paste as the base style prompt
in the ChatGPT fable-art-custom session):

> Soft watercolor children's picture-book illustration in the Tabitha
> Paige tradition: loose washes with visible pencil sketch lines beneath,
> botanical detail (ferns, wildflowers, birch leaves, moss), warm cream
> paper texture, unhurried compositions with generous negative space,
> palette of moss green, sage, dusty rose, warm terracotta, cream, and a
> hazy dawn-blue for windows; tender everyday domestic scenes; a
> newborn-welcome mood — quiet, hopeful, tenderly hand-made. No text,
> no watermarks, no logos, no photorealism, no harsh contrast, no
> modern technology on-page beyond one small warm lamp.

## Voice note (read before per-page direction)

This book is a **letter from Uncle Manav to Baby Pas** — first-person,
direct address, warm and specific. The illustrations are still literal
scenes (mama, papa, home, the coming-baby) — no letter-paper motif,
no envelope craft, no visible handwriting. The text carries the
letter-ness; the art carries the family life the letter is describing.

## Per-page scene direction

- **Page 1 — "Dear Baby Pas — I am writing this before I ever meet you…"**
  A single small warm lamp glowing in a window at dusk, the rest of the
  house dim. From the outside, a garden with wildflowers and a fence.
  No people on-page. The "light turning on" opens the letter — a room
  being prepared for her, seen from the sender's distance.
- **Page 2 — "…your mama is walking softly around the house, humming.
  Your papa is fixing a small thing."**
  A gentle interior with mama moving through the frame (soft steps,
  humming, one hand on the wall as she passes) — or mama in the rocking
  chair by the window, humming. Either reading of the existing art is
  fine; the text names both parents but the visible scene is mama's
  humming. Papa's small fixing is off-page in the same house — implied
  by warmth and light, not shown.
- **Page 3 — "Your papa is my best friend. He is kind, he is thoughtful,
  and he fixes the small thing before you even notice it needed fixing."**
  Papa crouched on the nursery threshold with a small screwdriver on
  the doorstep step, sleeves rolled. On a low shelf inside the nursery,
  the little lamp he has just adjusted glows warm. Toolbox open beside
  him — modest, cared-for. This is the "gadgets like her dad" beat —
  it is *tenderness expressed through tinkering*, not a gadget montage.
  Manav's voice is the frame; papa is the subject.
- **Page 4 — "Before you were ever a thought, your papa was already
  practicing — on my son, Azad…"**
  Papa (Clevon) in a warm ordinary moment with Azad — Azad is a
  brown-skinned boy, drawn from behind or in profile, small enough
  for Papa's arm around his shoulder or Papa lifting him. **Azad's face
  is not the subject; his being-with-Papa is.** Options: Papa and Azad
  looking at something together (a book, a small project on a table),
  Papa listening while Azad talks, or Papa carrying Azad on his shoulders
  in a garden. Warm afternoon light, cream/moss palette, unfussy.
  Azad's back turned or three-quarter away from viewer is preferred —
  he is a real living child (Manav's son) so we don't preempt his face
  either.
- **Page 5 — "Your mama is the kind of person who makes a room feel
  welcome the moment she walks in…"**
  Mama in a doorway or an entryway, warm light behind her, arms softly
  open or one hand extended in greeting — the visible verb is
  *welcoming*. Alternate: mama seated at a kitchen table with two mugs,
  passing one across to a friend (the friend's back to the viewer,
  unnamed and unpainted with detail — could be read as Uncle Manav in
  the past). Painterly, warm palette, generous negative space.
- **Page 6 — "Together, your mama and papa built a home that was ready
  for you long before you got here…"**
  Mama and papa together in the ordinary domestic frame that IS the
  home they built — cooking side-by-side, sharing tea on a small couch,
  laughing about something at a table. The point of the scene is the
  *ease between them*. Fine to re-purpose the earlier "mama with a
  swaddled bundle" composition as the coda of this page (mama holding
  the not-yet-baby bundle while papa stands nearby with a hand on her
  shoulder), if the text-fit reads better that way.
- **Page 7 — "So here is what I am, Baby Pas — Uncle Manav. I have been
  there for your papa. He has been there for me. And I plan to be there
  for you. Not in a big way — in the way that shows up on ordinary
  Tuesdays."**
  A soft everyday domestic scene that reads as *the promise being kept*:
  papa in the rocking chair with the swaddled baby on his shoulder,
  peaceful. Manav is not shown on-page (this is his voice, not his
  portrait). The image is what papa's life looks like now that the
  promise is real — a Tuesday, an ordinary hour, a small head where a
  head fits.
- **Page 8 — "You are new to this world, Baby Pas. But you are being
  handed to two of the best people I know…"**
  Family group — Mama and Papa on a couch, baby swaddled between them,
  the small bundle turned inward toward their bodies (baby's face away
  from viewer). Lamp lit, garden dusk outside the window. A wreath of
  wildflowers in the top margin as a soft border. This is the book's
  signature — small composition, lots of paper, cream border. The
  closing "— Uncle Manav" is spoken by the narrator, not lettered on
  the art.

## Consistency lock-ins (across all 8 pages)

Once the cover style is approved, these must stay identical page-to-page:

- **The nursery lamp:** small, warm, one specific shape — pick it on the
  cover and repeat it in every page that shows the nursery.
- **The window plant:** one potted fern, same pot (cream ceramic), same
  place beside the chair, on every page that shows the room.
- **Mama's cardigan:** cream or moss green, same garment across her pages.
- **Papa's shirt:** soft henley or button-down, sleeves rolled, same
  garment across his pages.
- **The swaddle:** cream muslin with a moss-green pattern (small
  botanical repeat — ferns or leaves). Same swaddle every time the baby
  is on-page.
- **Palette:** cream paper, moss green, sage, dusty rose, warm
  terracotta, hazy dawn-blue. No neon, no primary red, no bright yellow.
- **House exterior / window view:** private Flatbush Brooklyn house, near
  Flatbush Junction / Brooklyn College in feeling but never showing
  address details. Use light gray-white siding, shallow stoop, low masonry
  fence and railings, narrow driveway or gated side passage, close
  neighboring houses, softened overhead wires, and modest city planting
  beds. Through nursery windows, show leafy street trees and Brooklyn
  residential texture, not a rural birch grove.

## No readable text — anywhere

The `fable-art-custom` skill forbids visible text in generated images.
No lettering on the picture book on the shelf (page 4), no writing on the
side of the lamp, no baby-name on the crib (there isn't one yet anyway),
no words on any signage. The narrator carries all the words.

## Must NOT contain

Detailed baby face (see cast rule). Modern technology on-page beyond one
small warm lamp (no phones, no laptops, no baby monitor — "gadgets like
her dad" is delivered through Papa's *tenderness of tinkering*, not
through devices). Text of any kind. Photorealism. Harsh contrast. Any
person other than Mama, Papa, and (softly, wrapped) Baby Pas — no
siblings, grandparents, doctor, friends. No hospital scene — this book
is the home welcoming her, not the delivery room.

## Reference notes

- **Inspiration** — Tabitha Paige for palette and looseness. Nancy
  Tillman *On the Night You Were Born* for the arc register. Emily
  Winfield Martin *The Wonderful Things You Will Be* for the tender
  litany. Do not copy compositions — use these to *tune the mood*.
- **The gift context** — this is a gift from Uncle Manav to friends
  who are soon-to-be parents. The book is what he is giving them:
  proof that their child is already loved by the people around them.
  Illustration should feel like it was made *for a specific baby who
  is not here yet* — not a template with the name swapped in.
- **Delivery** — recipient family gets a gift certificate + magic URL
  at delivery time, not the URL directly at order time. See
  `docs/commerce/delivery-flow.md`.
