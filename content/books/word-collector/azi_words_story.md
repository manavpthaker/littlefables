# The Word Collector
*An Azi-Verse Story*

---

"Whisker-wiggle roll call!" Azi announced from his spot on the circular rug. His guitar leaned against the felt-covered thinking stool, and the afternoon light made his piano hum softly.

Jujy stretched his black-and-white paws. "Present and ready for—"

"That's so STUPID!" Dory blurted out, then immediately looked confused. "Wait, what's stupid? I forgot what I was talking about."

Azi's eyes widened. He'd heard that word before. It felt big in his mouth, like it might be too heavy. "Stupid," he repeated slowly, testing how it sounded.

"What big words are we learning in there?" Mama's voice drifted in from the kitchen, where something delicious was sizzling.

Azi picked up his guitar and strummed his thinking chord—C to G to A minor. Sometimes music helped him figure things out. "Mama, why do some words feel... bigger than others?"

Pandies spoke up from his corner, slow and thoughtful as always. "Words are like seeds, little one. Some grow flowers. Some grow weeds."

"I don't understand," Azi said, settling cross-legged with his guitar.

Clappy bounced excitedly. "Yesterday when Brady said my drumming sounded 'amazing,' I felt all sparkly inside! But when someone called Flo's garden 'messy,' she looked sad even though her flowers are beautiful."

Dory nodded enthusiastically, then looked puzzled. "Wait, what were we talking about?"

Jujy cleared his throat importantly. "Words are tools, obviously. Like... like..." He paused, having forgotten his own explanation.

"Like Papa's guitar," Azi said quietly, strumming again. "He can make it sound angry or gentle or happy, depending on how he plays."

"Exactly!" Mama appeared in the doorway with flour on her hands. "Words have their own rhythm and power, mi amor."

Slothie moved—very, very slowly—from the bookshelf to the rug, with Baby Slothie clinging to his arm. "Some... words... are... for... building," he said in his careful way. "Some... are... for... breaking."

Azi thought about this. "Like when Lito calls me 'mi nieto' on video calls? That builds something warm in my chest. And when Papa says 'I'm proud of you' after I practice piano, even when I mess up."

Citie, who'd been listening quietly, spoke up. "But that word Dory said—stupid—that one felt like it wanted to knock something down."

"Even though I didn't mean it to!" Dory added quickly.

Pandies nodded slowly. "Intent matters. But impact matters too."

Azi strummed his guitar thoughtfully. "So some words are like tools for fixing, and some are like tools for breaking?"

"And some," said Mama, settling onto the rug with them, "are just words that grow bigger in little mouths than they're meant to be."

"I want to be a word collector," Azi announced. "I want to collect the building words."

"What about the breaking words?" Clappy asked.

Azi considered this seriously. "Maybe we don't have to collect those ones. Maybe we can just leave them where we found them."

"But what if they slip out anyway?" Dory wondered.

Mama smiled. "Then we practice saying 'I'm sorry' and we try again. Words aren't magic spells. They're practice."

"Practice for what?" Azi asked.

"For becoming the kind of person you want to be. Someone who builds beautiful things with their words."

Jujy puffed up proudly. "I want to be a word warrior! Someone who fights for good feelings!"

"Word warriors don't fight," Pandies corrected gently. "They protect. They build. They heal."

As the sun started to set through Azi's playroom window, he began strumming a new pattern on his guitar.

"Want to help me collect building words?" he asked his companions.

"Gentle!" called Dory.

"Strong!" said Jujy.

"Patient," whispered Slothie.

"Kind and beautiful and brave," Baby Slothie added.

"Music," said Azi. "And family. And..." He paused, thinking. "How do you say 'I choose my words carefully'?"

Mama's voice was warm with pride. "I choose my words carefully. Or if you want to practice Spanish: Elijo mis palabras con cuidado."

Azi repeated it slowly, tasting each syllable. The words felt different in his mouth than "stupid" had. They felt purposeful. Strong but gentle. Like good tools in careful hands.

At dinner, Papa noticed the way Azi paused before speaking, the way he seemed to be listening to his own words before letting them out.

"How was your day, hijo?" Papa asked, passing the arepas.

"I learned that words are like music," Azi said thoughtfully. "And I want to make good music with mine."

"What kind of music?" Mama asked with a smile.

"The kind that makes people feel like they belong. Like when Dadi sings those songs that sound like hugs even though I don't understand all the words yet."

Papa reached across and squeezed Azi's hand. "That's very wise, my word warrior."

Outside, the first stars were appearing. Through the window, the moon was beginning its nightly watch over all the children of the world—children learning to choose their words like instruments, learning to build instead of break, learning that the strongest superpower isn't the loudest voice, but the most thoughtful one.

Azi strummed his guitar one more time before bed, and in the gentle evening air, his music sounded like words, and his words sounded like love.
