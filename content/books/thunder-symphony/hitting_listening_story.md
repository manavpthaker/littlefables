# Azi and the Thunder Symphony

Azi sat on his circular rug, pressing the same three chords on his AKAI keyboard over and over. C-G-Am. C-G-Am. But today, nothing sounded right. Each note came out harsh and angry, like the music itself was fighting with him.

"Why won't you work?" he muttered, his fingers pounding harder on the keys. Around him, his friends watched quietly—Jujy with his confident black-and-white stripes, dreamy Dory, and gentle Citie sitting close by, always ready to listen.

The tangled feelings in Azi's chest grew hotter and bigger until—SMACK!—his hand swept out and struck Citie, who tumbled backward with a small, hurt sound.

The playroom fell silent except for one deep note echoing from the keyboard.

Azi stared at his hands, then at Citie's surprised face. His friend's eyes glistened with tears, and his small voice trembled as he asked, "Why did you hit me, Azi? I was just sitting here."

"I... I didn't mean to," Azi whispered, feeling his anger drain away and leave something cold and heavy behind. "I'm sorry, Citie. I was mad at the music, not at you."

But that deep note from the keyboard began to grow and shimmer, spreading through the air like ripples in a pond. The walls of the playroom started to fade and blur.

**The Thunder Mountains**

Suddenly, Azi found himself standing in a vast landscape where the ground beneath his feet thrummed with rhythm. Enormous timpani drums stretched before him like mountains, their copper surfaces gleaming in purple storm light. Each drum was as tall as a house, and they rolled with deep, rumbling beats that shook the ground and made his teeth rattle.

"Welcome to the Thunder Mountains," said a voice behind him.

Azi turned to see a wise-looking conductor with silver hair and a baton that sparkled like starlight. The conductor's eyes were kind but serious. "Every feeling has its own music, young musician. And every music has its own land. This is where anger lives."

As if summoned by his words, the timpani mountains erupted in a thunderous symphony. BOOM! CRASH! BANG! The sound was enormous and wild, shaking loose rocks and sending them tumbling down the mountainsides. Azi could feel the anger music in his bones—the same hot, pounding feeling he'd had right before he hit Citie.

"It's so loud," Azi said, covering his ears. "And it makes everything shake."

"Yes," the conductor nodded. "Thunder is powerful. But what happens when thunder crashes into tender places?"

**The Valley of Tender Hearts**

The conductor led Azi down a winding path to a completely different landscape. Here, violin strings stretched across a gentle valley like silver grass, trembling in the breeze. When the wind touched them, they sang with voices so soft and sweet they made Azi's heart ache.

But as the thunder from the mountains rolled down into the valley, the delicate strings shivered and cried out in pain. Some snapped with tiny, breaking sounds that were somehow worse than the loudest crash.

"This is where your friend Citie's feelings live," the conductor explained gently. "Can you hear how your thunder sounds when it reaches here?"

Azi listened, and his stomach twisted. The beautiful violin valley was full of frightened, hurt sounds—exactly like the small cry Citie had made when Azi hit him.

"I didn't know my anger could travel this far," Azi said, his voice small. "I didn't know it could hurt the tender music."

"Most people don't realize," the conductor said. "That's why we're going to visit one more place."

**The Listening Gardens**

They walked through a gate made of intertwined flute bodies into the most peaceful place Azi had ever seen. Here, hundreds of wind chimes hung from trees, each one a different size and shape. Some were tiny bells that tinkled like fairy laughter. Others were deep, wooden tubes that hummed like gentle giants. When the breeze moved through them, they created the most beautiful harmony Azi had ever heard.

"In the Listening Gardens," the conductor explained, "every voice matters. Even the quietest whisper becomes part of the symphony. Close your eyes and listen carefully."

Azi closed his eyes and listened with his whole body. Beneath all the chimes, he could hear something else—a steady, gentle rhythm like a faithful heartbeat. It had been playing all along, but the thunder had been too loud for him to notice.

"That's Citie's song," he breathed. "It's so kind and steady. It was there the whole time, wasn't it?"

"It was," the conductor smiled. "And what if your thunder learned to play *with* that heartbeat instead of over it?"

Azi tried it in his mind. Instead of wild, crashing beats, he imagined his anger drums playing gently along with Citie's rhythm—boom-tap, boom-tap, boom-tap. Like rain on a roof instead of a hurricane.

Something magical happened. The violin strings stopped trembling and began to sing. The wind chimes joined in with tiny, silver voices. Even the thunder drums softened into a steady rhythm that held everything together instead of breaking it apart.

"This is the Symphony of Understanding," the conductor said. "When all the instruments listen to each other, they can make music that's bigger and more beautiful than any one of them alone."

**Coming Home**

The musical landscape began to fade like morning mist, and Azi found himself back in his playroom. In his hand was a small, shimmering tuning fork.

"When thunder comes again," the conductor's voice whispered from far away, "strike this first. It will remind you to listen for the other instruments."

Citie was still sitting nearby, a sad expression on his face. Azi struck the tuning fork gently—ping!—and listened. There it was: Citie's heartbeat rhythm, steady and forgiving even though he was hurt.

"Citie," Azi said, crawling across the rug, "I'm really sorry I hit you. I heard your song in the Listening Gardens, and it's beautiful. I want to learn to play my thunder feelings *with* your music, not over it."

Citie's eyes brightened a little. "What do you mean?"

"I mean..." Azi thought carefully. "Maybe when I get angry, I could tell you with words instead of hands? And maybe you could help me remember that my feelings are like music—they're okay to have, but I need to be careful how I play them around other people."

"I'd like that," Citie said softly. "Could we practice? Maybe we could make a friendship symphony where all our different feelings have a place?"

Azi smiled and placed his hands gently on his keyboard. This time, he started by listening—to Citie's breathing, to the sounds of the house around them, to the quiet music that connected them all. When he finally played his three chords, they sounded completely different. They sounded like coming home.

From that day forward, whenever storm clouds gathered in Azi's chest, he remembered the Thunder Symphony and reached for his tuning fork first. And in the playroom where friends gathered each morning, their music—gentle and strong, sorry and forgiving—played on.
