# The Moon Who Knew About Time

*An Azi-Verse Story*

*For everyone still learning that a day has edges*

---

## Part One: The Hour That Belonged to Him

It was Friday, and Friday was the good one.

The Westfield sat the way it always sat — slightly sideways to the sun, green clapboard, red door, curtains whispering when the wind moved just right. Outside, the garden was doing its slow summer work. Inside, the whole house smelled like dinner and the day cooling down.

And on the playroom floor, half-finished, sat the rocket.

It had been the box the new chair came in. Papa had cut a door in the side with the good scissors, the sharp ones Azi wasn't allowed to touch. Azi had drawn the dials himself — forty-one of them, because forty-one was a number he liked the shape of. Three days of work. Three days of coming back to it.

All that was left was the nose cone and the name on the side.

"After dinner," Papa had said, "we finish the rocket."

Azi had nodded so hard his curls bounced.

"One hour," Papa said. "Then bath. Then teeth. Then story. Deal?"

"Deal," said Azi.

And he meant it. That is the thing about promises when you are four. He meant it all the way down.

*

But an hour is a strange animal.

An hour looks enormous when it is standing in front of you. It looks like the backyard. It looks like the whole backyard and the garden and the deck and probably Flo's house across the way too.

And then you turn around, and it is behind you.

Azi went to get the good markers.

On the way to the good markers, he found the blue truck that had been missing since Tuesday.

The blue truck needed to go with the other trucks. Obviously. But the other trucks were not lined up right — some facing the window, some facing the door, all of them crooked, which was wrong, which was *unbearable*.

So he lined them up. All eleven of them. Every nose pointing the same way. Exactly even.

It took a long time. It felt like nothing at all.

Then Clappy had to be introduced to the trucks, one by one, with names.

Then there was a song in his hands that would not leave until he played it, so he played it, four times, because the fourth one was the good one.

Then Jujy was missing and had to be found under the blanket, except it was Dory under the blanket, which was so funny that being funny took a while too.

*(How much of the hour do you think is left? Guess out loud before you turn the page.)*

*

"Azi. Bath, *mi amor*."

Azi looked up.

The rocket sat exactly where it had been sitting for three days. No nose cone. No name.

"Wait," he said. "Wait — no. We didn't do it yet."

Mama crouched down to his eye level, the way she does. "It's seven-thirty, sweetheart."

"But Papa SAID an hour."

"He gave you the hour."

And that was the worst part.

Because Azi looked at the clock he could not read yet, and at the eleven trucks lined up perfectly even, and at the rocket with no nose, and something in him understood — not with words, with his whole stomach — that nobody had taken the hour away from him.

He had spent it.

Every single minute of it. On things he could not even remember deciding to do.

---

## Part Two: When It Ran Out

"NO."

The word came out of him like something thrown.

"No bath. No teeth. I want the ROCKET."

"Tomorrow we'll do the nose cone," Papa said. "First thing. I promise —"

"I don't WANT tomorrow!"

His hands were already moving. They found the box — the good box, with the door cut so carefully and the forty-one dials — and shoved.

It went over sideways. The whole left side crumpled inward with a sound like a stepped-on leaf.

Azi heard it crumple.

He heard it, and he kept going anyway, because the bigness was already in the room, and the bigness never knocks.

> "You RUINED it!" Azi shouted. "You ruined it and I don't want you here!"

Papa did not shout back. Papa never shouts back.

He crouched down, slow, the way he does when things get hard. And he said, very quietly:

"Plant your feet, *beta*."

*

And Azi — even then, even with his face hot and his fists still wanting — remembered the moose.

He planted his feet.

He felt for the roots going down. Down through the playroom rug, down through the floorboards, down under the green house into the dark cool dirt where nothing hurries.

He breathed in for four. One... two... three... four.

He breathed out for six.

And the bigness got smaller. It really did. It went from a shout to a hum to almost nothing, the way a summer storm walks off toward somebody else's town.

The moose had been right about the bigness.

*

But underneath the bigness was something the moose had never said one word about.

It wasn't hot. It was cold.

It was flat and heavy and hollow, and it sat down in the middle of Azi's chest and would not get up, and it said the same thing over and over in a voice that sounded a little bit like his own:

*You always do this.*

*You always run out.*

Bath happened. Teeth happened. But by then the clock said what clocks say, and there was no story. Not tonight.

Mama kissed the top of his head anyway. "Mañana, *mi cielo*. Tomorrow."

"Okay," said Azi.

But he did not believe in tomorrow just then.

Tomorrow felt like one more thing he would spend without noticing.

---

## Part Three: The Window and the Watching

Azi did not sleep.

He sat up in his twilight-colored room, on the little sofa with the slumped companions, with his knees pulled up inside his soft blue rocket pajamas, and he looked out the perfect moon-view window.

Downstairs, the rocket was lying on its side with its ribs caved in.

> "I never have enough," Azi whispered to the glass.

The window fogged a little where his breath touched it.

> "There's never enough time. Not ever. Not for anybody."

He said it quiet and certain, the way you say things that feel too true to argue with.

*

And the moon was there.

That was not the strange part. The moon is always there. The moon had looked in this window every night of Azi's life. It had watched him be a baby who could not hold his own head up. It had watched him learn to stand, and fall, and stand. It had watched a thousand goodnights in three different languages and understood every one.

The strange part was that tonight the moon was *close*.

Not a little closer. Close the way a face is close.

It filled the whole window, edge to edge. Grey and gold and old. And all over it — everywhere, thousands of them — circles. Circles inside circles. Rings inside rings. Like a pond in the rain that had frozen halfway through the splash and never once thawed.

Azi did not call Mama.

He did not call Papa.

He did not move at all.

Something told him this was a moment that needed to stay quiet.

Something told him this was a moment that was just for him.

*(Take a slow breath here. In through your nose... out through your mouth...)*

And then — the way things only happen at the window of a green house with a red door, very late, when a boy has spent every hour he had — the moon spoke.

---

## Part Four: The Voice in the Quiet

Not out loud.

Not in words that made a sound.

But somehow, in the space between Azi's thoughts — in that quiet place where wishes live and dreams get made — a voice arrived.

It was low and unhurried, like the last note of a song still living in the string after the string has stopped moving. Like snow deciding to fall. Like a whole room remembering the day it just had.

> *You think you wasted it.*

Azi's breath caught. He pressed his palm flat against the cold glass.

> *I watched you today.*

The voice took its time. It was in no hurry whatsoever.

> *I saw you line up eleven trucks so that every single one faced the same way. Nobody asked you to do that. You did it because you wanted it to be right.*

Azi felt something loosen, just slightly, behind his ribs.

> *I saw you find Dory under the blanket instead of Jujy, and laugh, and let the laugh have its whole entire size.*

He remembered. He had laughed until he had to sit down.

> *I saw a song get stuck in your hands and refuse to leave until you played it. You played it four times. The fourth one was the good one.*

"How did you know that?"

> *Because I was watching. That is the only thing I do.*

The moon's light shifted a little in the window.

> *Azi. Listen. That was not wasting.*
>
> *That was spending.*

*

"Then why does it feel so bad?" Azi's voice was very small.

> *Because you did not choose. And the hour did not wait around to be chosen.*

Azi pressed his forehead to the glass.

"It goes so fast," he whispered. "That's what nobody tells you. It goes so fast and I can't *feel* it going. Not even a little. It just — it's there, and then somebody says a number, and it's gone."

A tear went down his cheek. He had not felt it coming.

"And there's never anything left for the thing I wanted most. The rocket. I wanted the rocket most out of everything and it got *nothing*."

> *I know.*

The voice was gentler now. Almost tender.

> *I know about time.*

---

## Part Five: What the Craters Are

The moon came closer still — so close that Azi could see into the circles, could see that some of the rings had smaller rings inside them, and smaller ones inside those, going down and down like a story with a story in it.

> *When I was young, I did not know how to spend either.*

The voice had changed. It was still slow. But there was something new in it now — something that sounded like remembering.

> *I wanted to see everything. All of it. Every star, every edge, every dark corner of the whole sky.*
>
> *So I turned.*

"You turned?"

> *I spun. Faster and faster and faster, showing every side of myself to everything at once, looking in all directions so that I would not miss a single thing.*

The moon was quiet for a moment.

> *And I saw the whole sky, Azi.*
>
> *And I saw nothing. Not one thing. Not one single thing all the way through.*

*

Azi looked at the circles. There were so many.

"What are those?" he asked. "The holes."

> *Ah.*
>
> *While I was turning, things came. Out of the dark, fast and burning, one after another after another. And every one of them asked the same question.*

"What question?"

> *Can I land here.*

The moon's light dimmed, just slightly.

> *And I said yes. To every single one. Not because I wanted to — because I was spinning too fast to say anything else. Yes, and yes, and yes, and yes.*
>
> *I never once chose, Azi. So everything chose me instead.*

Azi stared. Thousands of them. Ring inside ring, all the way to the edge.

> *These are not wounds. Everyone thinks they are wounds.*
>
> *They are the nights I did not decide.*

*(Look at the moon tonight, if you can. Every circle is a night somebody didn't choose.)*

*

"So what did you do?"

> *I stopped turning.*

The moon said it simply, the way you say a thing you have been sure about for a very long time.

> *I picked one thing to look at. And I have looked at it, all the way through, every night since. The same face. Always. Forever.*

"What did you pick?"

> *You.*

Azi's hand slipped off the glass.

> *Your green house with the red door. Your window. This one. Your mother's window when she was small and far away in another country, and her mother's window before that. Every child in every language who has ever looked up and felt better.*
>
> *I gave up the entire rest of the sky to do that properly.*

The moon's voice went very quiet.

> *There is a side of me you have never seen. Not once. No one on your whole world has ever seen it, not in all the years there have been people.*
>
> *That is what it cost.*

Azi thought about that for a long time.

"Are you sad about it?"

> *No.*
>
> *And that, Azi, is the whole difference between losing time and spending it.*

---

## Part Six: Three Lights

> *Come up. It is easier to show you.*

"Come *up*?"

> *You have a rocket.*

"It's broken. I broke it."

> *It is a box with a door in it. It was always a box with a door in it. That was never what made it work.*

*

So Azi went down the stairs quiet as a sock, past Mama and Papa talking low in the living room, into the playroom where the rocket lay on its side with its ribs caved in.

He set it upright.

He climbed in through the door Papa had cut.

And then, because there are rules about these things, he cupped his hands around his mouth and said the words that start everything:

> "Whisker-wiggle roll call!"

*

They came the way they always come. Immediately, and as if they had been waiting all along.

"Jujy. Commander."

"Monkie. Checklist."

"Clappy. Music."

"Dory. Lookout."

"Lookout for what?" said Dory.

"Pandies. Mission Control."

Pandies settled into the seat by the forty-one dials and put on his invisible ranger's hat. "*Todos listos?*" he asked. Everyone ready?

Azi put his hands on the keyboard he had drawn in marker, and played the three chords that live in his fingers.

C.

G.

A-minor.

And the playroom floor let go.

*

The moon, when you arrive at it, is not a light. It is a place. It is grey and soft and covered in dust that has been sitting still for longer than there have been words for how long.

Monkie handed Azi a suit.

On the chest of the suit were three small round lights, in a row, glowing warm.

"Three lights," said Pandies, calm in his ear the way Mission Control is calm. "That is what the suit holds. When the last one goes out, the suit brings you home. It will not ask you first."

"How long is a light?"

"A light is a light," said Pandies. "You will feel it."

And the suit hummed its first note.

**C.** Warm and wide open, like a whole Saturday.

---

## Part Seven: The Door and the Rock

Azi ran for the airlock.

"Not yet," said Monkie, who had a checklist and a great deal of patience. "Gloves. Helmet. Tether."

"But I want to *go*."

"I know."

Azi bounced on his heels while Monkie sealed the gloves, one and then the other. He sighed enormously while the helmet clicked. He nearly came out of his skin while Monkie clipped the tether to his belt — slowly, checking it twice, the way Monkie checks everything.

And the whole time, the door stayed shut.

Not because anyone was being unkind. Not because anyone was cross with him.

The airlock did not open for wanting.

It opened for done.

*

Then it opened, and there was the moon underfoot, and Azi forgot every single thing.

He bounced.

He bounced high, and higher, and highest, and the dust came up around his boots in slow grey clouds that took forever to come down. He wrote his name in the dust with one finger. He bounced again. He bounced *again*.

The suit changed its note.

**G.**

Two lights.

And Azi stopped, because he had just remembered why he came.

*

There was a crater he had picked out from his bedroom window, ages ago, back when he still thought of the moon as a picture and not a place. The good one. The one shaped a little like a bowl.

He had a plan about that crater. He was going to bring home a rock from it. A real one. For the shelf.

He got there and it was full of shadow.

Not a little shadow. All shadow. Filled to the brim with it, dark as the inside of a pocket.

He could not see a single stone.

Not one.

"That's not fair," said Azi.

And it wasn't.

*

Clappy sat down in the dust beside him and felt it out loud, the way Clappy does, with his whole round self.

Slothie came slow, and stayed close, and said nothing at all.

Nobody told him not to be angry.

*(If you were standing there right now — what would you want to do?)*

Then Pandies, quiet in his ear:

"Two lights, Azi. What now?"

*

Azi looked at the shadow for a long time.

The old thing wanted to happen. He could feel it waiting: the shove, the sharp word, the bigness that comes when a plan breaks.

He planted his feet.

He breathed in for four, out for six.

And then, because the bigness was handled and there was still a problem left over, he did the new thing.

He looked up.

*

There were rocks everywhere.

There always had been. The whole moon was rocks. He had been standing on a hundred thousand of them the entire time, staring into one hole.

Just not the ones he had planned on.

Azi picked one up. Small. Grey. A little bit sparkly if you turned it toward the light.

The suit hummed its last note. **A-minor** — the one that sounds like almost-evening.

One light.

And Azi was very, very far from the door.

*

But the tether was clipped.

The boring tether. The slow one. The one that had taken so long that he had nearly come out of his skin.

It gathered him up and brought him home like a song bringing you to the end of itself, and the airlock closed, and the last light went out exactly then — not one second early, not one second late.

He had not bounced every bounce he wanted to bounce.

He had not written his name twice.

He had the rock.

---

## Part Eight: The Return

The playroom floor came back the way it always does, as if it had never been anywhere.

Azi climbed out through the door Papa had cut, with a small grey rock in his fist, and stood for a moment in the quiet.

Then he went and found Mama and Papa on the couch, talking low the way grown-ups do when they are trying to work something out.

When they saw him in the doorway, they stopped, and they opened their arms.

He walked over. Not running. Not crashing. Just walking, one foot and then the other, and climbed into the Azi-shaped space between them that always seems to be waiting no matter what.

*

"I'm sorry about the rocket," he said.

His voice was small but the words were real.

"My hands did something my heart didn't mean."

Papa's hand smoothed his curls — the same hand that had caught his fists two hours ago. "Thank you for telling us, *beta*."

"And I'm sorry about the hour." Azi thought about how to say the next part. "I didn't lose it. I spent it. On trucks."

Mama laughed, once, softly, and kissed the top of his head. "That is a very good sentence, *mi cielo*."

"The moon told me."

Mama and Papa looked at each other over the top of his head. Something passed between them that was not doubt at all.

It looked a great deal like wonder.

"What else did the moon tell you?" Papa asked.

*

So Azi told them.

"There's three things," he said, and held up three fingers, one at a time.

"**Door first.**" He put down a finger. "Some doors don't open for wanting. They open for done. That's teeth and bath and shoes. It's not because you're mean. It's just how doors are."

"**One thing first.**" Another finger. "Before you spend it, you have to say out loud the one thing you'd be saddest to miss. And then you do *that* one first. Not last. First."

"**Look up.**" The last finger. "When the thing you planned on is gone — when it's all shadow and there's nothing in it — you look up. There's always another rock. It's just not the one you picked."

Papa was very quiet.

"Say the second one again," he said.

"One thing first."

"Okay," said Papa. "Then I'll go. Tomorrow, my one thing is the nose cone. Before anything else. Before coffee."

"Before *coffee*?"

"Before coffee."

Mama held up her hand. "Mine is the garden. Fifteen minutes. First."

And Azi thought about it seriously, the way he thinks about puzzles, turning it over to find the corner pieces.

"Mine is the name," he said. "On the side of the rocket. I want to do the name."

*

They put the rock on the shelf in the playroom, next to the record player, where the moon coming through the window would find it every single night.

Then Papa taped the crumpled side of the rocket from the inside, so the tape would not show.

It still had a dent.

Everybody agreed that a rocket that has actually been somewhere ought to.

---

## Part Nine: The Dreaming

That night Azi fell asleep in about four minutes.

Mama tucked the soft blanket around him. Papa kissed his forehead and whispered the words he always whispers.

"Good night. Sweet dreams. I'll see you in the morning. I love you to infinity and *más allá*."

Azi closed his eyes.

And he dreamed.

*

He dreamed of dust that took forever to settle.

He dreamed of eleven trucks all facing the same way, which was, he still felt, worth it.

He dreamed of a crater full of shadow, and of looking up, and of rocks all the way to the edge of everywhere.

He dreamed of three small lights on the chest of a suit, going out one at a time, in order, the way they are supposed to.

*

And somewhere very high above the green house with the red door, the moon held still.

It did not turn. It has not turned in a very long time.

Its craters lay quiet across its face — thousands of them, ring inside ring — all the old nights it had not chosen, kept where it could see them, the way you keep a thing you have learned from.

And it looked down through one bedroom window at one sleeping boy, all the way through, giving him the whole of its one good face.

It had given up the entire rest of the sky for this.

And it was glad.

*The End*

# The Three Lights

*A Practice Guide for Real Days*

This story plants language during calm that can be used when the day gets away from you. After a few readings, "one thing first" does the work that "hurry up" never did.

---

## The Three Steps

**1. Door first.**
Some things open the way to other things — teeth, shoes, shower, seatbelt. Framing them as doors rather than demands moves the non-negotiable out of the parent and into the world, where a child can accept it without losing face.

**2. One thing first.**
Before unstructured time, ask for *the one thing you'd be saddest to miss* — then do that one first, not last. Four-year-olds cannot forecast an hour, but they can absolutely rank one wish above another. Ordering is the part of time management they can actually do.

**3. Look up.**
When the plan breaks, the instinct is to keep staring into the hole. "Look up" is a physical instruction, which is why it works: it moves the body before it argues with the feeling.

---

## In the Moment

- "How many lights do we have?" *(Better than "hurry up" — it's a quantity, not a scold.)*
- "What's your one thing? Let's do that one first."
- "The door opens for done."
- "It's all shadow in there. Look up — what else is there?"

---

## After the Hour

- Name the difference without blame: **"We didn't lose it. We spent it. On trucks."** Spending is a choice with dignity in it. Losing is a failure. Same hour, completely different story about who he is.
- Let the spending be honest sometimes: lining up eleven trucks *was* worth it to him. The lesson isn't that play is waste — it's that the thing you love most should go first.
- Model it out loud: "My one thing today was ___, and I did it first." Then let him hear you get it wrong, too.

---

## Preventive Use

- Before any open stretch: "Name your one thing."
- Before a transition: "Two lights, then bath."
- When a plan changes before it starts: "That crater's full of shadow today. Let's look up."

---

## The Key Phrases

- **"One thing first"** — ranking, not scheduling
- **"The door opens for done"** — the non-negotiable, without a villain
- **"Look up — there's another rock"** — adaptability, with a body instruction attached
- **"Did we lose it or spend it?"** — the whole book in one question

---

*The difference between losing time and spending it is only ever choosing.*
