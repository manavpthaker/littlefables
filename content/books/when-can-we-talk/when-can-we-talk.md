# When Can We Talk?

*For Lokjit, from Azad*

---

**1**

For four years, Lokjit and Azad saw each other every single day.

Every morning. Every snack. Every nap. Every afternoon.

They never once had to plan it.

---

**2**

Then Lokjit's family moved to a city on the other side of the world.

A real city, with real streets and a real school. Very, very far away.

So far that you have to sleep on an airplane to get there.

---

**3**

They still wanted to talk every day.

That part had not changed at all.

---

**4**

So Lokjit called.

The screen came on and showed a dark room, and a lump in the middle of a bed. The lump did not move.

"Azad?"

The lump kept sleeping.

---

**5**

So Azad called.

The screen came on and showed a dark room, and a lump in the middle of a bed. The lump did not move.

"Lokjit?"

That lump kept sleeping too.

---

**6**

They tried again. And again.

Breakfast. Nothing.

After school. Nothing.

Bath time. Nothing.

---

**7**

"Is he *EVER* awake?" said Azad.

"Is he *EVER* awake?" said Lokjit, at the exact same second, from the other side of the world, about Azad.

---

**8**

So Azad went to the window and asked the sun.

The sun would know. The sun goes everywhere.

---

**9**

"I can only be in one place at a time," said the sun. "People forget that about me."

---

**10**

"I get to Lokjit first. I am over his city while you are still fast asleep.

Then I come all the way here.

It takes me twelve hours."

---

**11**

"How much is twelve?"

"All of your fingers," said the sun. "And then two more."

---

**12**

"And when you're here?"

"Then I am not there," said the sun. "That is simply how it works. I'm sorry. It is a very big job."

---

**13**

That night, on the other side of the world, Lokjit asked the moon the same thing.

"I take whoever the sun is not doing," said the moon. "Right now that is Azad. He is asleep. He has been asleep for hours."

---

**14**

So now they both knew exactly what the problem was.

When Lokjit is awake, Azad is asleep.

When Azad is awake, Lokjit is asleep.

Their days do not line up.

---

**15**

Which left one question, and it was a big one.

*When can we talk?*

---

**16**

Lokjit worked on it from his end.

He stayed up ten minutes past his bedtime. Nothing.

He stayed up twenty minutes past. Still nothing.

---

**17**

Then one night he stayed up a little longer than that.

And there was Azad. On the screen. Awake. Holding a bowl of cereal.

---

**18**

"You're *eating*," said Lokjit.

"It's breakfast," said Azad.

"It's my *bedtime*."

They both thought this was the funniest thing that had ever happened to anybody.

---

**19**

That was the first one.

**Lokjit's bedtime is Azad's breakfast.**

---

**20**

Azad found the other one himself, a few days later.

He got out of the bath early. He put his pajamas on fast. He called.

And there was Lokjit, at a table, with the sun coming in the window behind him.

---

**21**

"*You're* eating," said Azad.

"It's breakfast," said Lokjit.

---

**22**

That was the second one.

**Azad's bedtime is Lokjit's breakfast.**

---

**23**

Two.

Out of a whole entire day, there were exactly two.

---

**24**

And here is the hard part.

Two is not very many, and neither one lasts very long.

---

**25**

On Monday, Azad found a truck under the couch on his way to breakfast. It had to be lined up with the other trucks. All of them. Facing the same way.

By the time he was finished, Lokjit was asleep.

---

**26**

On Tuesday, Lokjit took an extremely long time about his teeth.

Same thing. Gone.

---

**27**

Nobody had closed it on them. Nobody was being mean.

The window just closes by itself. That is what windows do.

---

**28**

So they made a rule.

The rule was: **call first.**

Not after the trucks. Not after the teeth. First.

---

**29**

Now Azad calls before the cereal instead of after it.

And Lokjit puts his pajamas on before he calls, so there is nothing left to do afterward.

You keep a window open by getting there early.

---

**30**

And once the calls started working, they found out something else.

The sun gets to Lokjit first. So Lokjit is already *in* the day while Azad is only just starting it.

---

**31**

So at breakfast, Azad asks:

"What's in it?"

And Lokjit, who has already been there, tells him.

"Wind. Wear the hood one."

---

**32**

And at bedtime, when Azad is finishing the day and Lokjit is only starting his, Lokjit asks:

"How'd it go?"

And Azad tells him.

"Good. There were a lot of leaves."

---

**33**

Lokjit takes the job seriously.

He warns about wind, and about assemblies, and about the days when the milk tastes funny.

Once he called just to say, "Don't wear the shoes with the buckles." He would not explain why.

He was usually right.

---

**34**

They send each other things, too.

Azad taught Lokjit three chords on the guitar. Lokjit taught Azad three taps with two fingers.

---

**35**

Some weeks they still miss one.

There is a birthday party. There is a long car ride with a nap in the middle of it.

---

**36**

When that happens, Azad asks the moon, who was working, and saw the whole thing.

"He got up slow," says the moon. "He ate an egg. He put his shoes on the wrong feet and did not notice until the door."

---

**37**

The moon notices things like that. The moon has an excellent memory and nothing else to do.

---

**38**

And Lokjit asks the sun, who covers a great deal of ground.

"Fell off the low wall. Did not cry. Drew a truck," says the sun, already leaving. "Big day. Lot of children. Next."

---

**39**

"Wait," says Lokjit. "Was he okay?"

The sun stops, which the sun does not like to do.

"He taps his fingers on things. Table. Bus window. His own knee. Three at a time. He does not seem to know he is doing it."

---

**40**

Lokjit knew exactly what that was.

He had taught it to him.

---

**41**

Twelve hours apart.

Two windows a day.

It is not very much.

---

**42**

But they have never once missed both.

---

**43**

"What's in it?"

"How'd it go?"
